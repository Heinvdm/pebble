# Fortifying Pebble against outside bad actors

## Owner's answers (20 October 2026)
1. **Bluetooth on the body:** off in normal use; a 2-minute pairing window opened by hand on the body.
2. **Body settings and reboot:** over USB only.
3. **Network:** Pebble goes on its own guest/IoT Wi-Fi with client isolation, and the phone has no SIM.
4. **What a voice alone may do:** everyday things only. Following, guarded places, alarm stand-downs and house actions need voice plus face with moving lips, or the family word.
5. **Who may stand down an alarm:** only someone Pebble knows, can see and hear, speaking to it and confirming; a fall alarm only by the person who fell, or a trusted person in view.
6. **Trust:** it may rise to "trusted" on its own; the inner circle is given by the owner only.
7. **Children:** never told where, or led to, medicines, knives, lighters, matches or chemicals; keys and valuables need a trusted person or family.
8. **A guest's "stop listening":** ends 10 minutes after they leave, at most 2 hours; "help" and alarms are always heard.
9. **eFuses on the ESP32:** not now (do it on the safety chip later).
10. **Notices:** skipped for now (the notifier stays off by default).
11. **ggwave bots:** always strangers, never trusted.
12. **Lost mode:** not now.


The owner asked (20 October 2026): "look into how we can fortify Pebble from outside bad actors."

This report is a security review of the code as it stands at the end of 20 October 2026. It was done by reading the firmware, the Android app and the brain, and by running small test scripts against copies of the code outside the repo. Nothing was tried against a real device or the home network. Every finding below names the file and line it comes from. Two reviewers read each area separately, and their findings agreed.

## In short

- **The body is the weakest point.** Anyone within Bluetooth range, or any device on the home Wi-Fi, can drive Pebble, switch off its drop and obstacle reflexes so the change survives a restart, and move the body onto their own Wi-Fi. No pairing or password is needed.
- **"Who asked" is often decided by words, not by evidence.** A child or guest who answers "who's asking?" with the owner's name gets the owner's rights. The model also names the asker in tool calls, and nothing checks that name against who really spoke.
- **Voice alone decides who someone is.** A recording, a voice clone, a TV, or a voice that sounds alike can pass as a known person, and a near match is then learnt as theirs.
- **Ordinary words can stand down a real alarm.** "Never mind" or "don't worry" from the TV can end a fall or smoke alarm and quiet that sign for up to an hour.
- **Some files become code.** A body profile sent to the phone can run any command as the app and switch off the smoke and fall safety. Model files are loaded without any check.
- **The seal itself is sound.** The encryption, the password stretching, the recovery cards, the locked room and the keystore key are well built. The weak spots are around it: password paths without a try limit, a house-settings check that never runs, and plain-text logs.

## Build these first

About four working days, in this order. Each step stands on its own.

1. **Close the body (about half a day).** BLE off unless a 2-minute pairing window is opened by hand. `C` (settings) and `R` (reboot) accepted over USB only. Tighter setting bounds: edge 40-150 mm, stop 30-300 mm, cpm 5-60 or 0, tilt inside -35 to 90, trim ±10, rest inside the tilt range. At most a few settings a second, and no write when the value hasn't changed. The brain reads `C ?` at every link-up and won't drive if edge, stop or cpm differ from what it expects. Put the phone and body on their own Wi-Fi network (router setting, half an hour).
2. **Make "who asked" a fact, not a word (about a day).** A claimed name works for "come here" only. Every tool call carries a requester set by code from the speech that started the turn; a named asker who isn't the requester is refused. `request_change`, `request_forget` and `introduce` leave background turns. Children and friends are never told where medicines, knives, lighters, matches or chemicals are.
3. **Stop files from becoming code (about a day).** The phone's body profile becomes an overlay that can set only the camera angles, `drive.cpm` and the wheel track. Model files must match a SHA-256 list in the APK. Profile and models move to internal storage. The receiver binds to the Wi-Fi address, takes the code in a header, and closes after one upload.
4. **Alarms that the TV can't end (about half a day).** Stand-downs only from words said to Pebble by a verified known person; a "help" incident only by the person who fell; re-raise if the beeping continues 30 s after a stand-down; separate budget for serious notices.
5. **Lock the phone down (about half a day).** adb blocked by default with a timed allow; the keeper ends an expired kiosk pause; the admin screen closes when idle; the missing restrictions; USB data off when the body is on Wi-Fi (test `canUsbDataSignalingBeDisabled()` on the Pixel first).
6. **Then:** the shared-key handshake on the body link (1-1.5 days), voice plus face plus lips for trust-bearing requests (1-2 days, once face prints are wired), the password try limit and log clean-up (half a day).

## Decisions for the owner

Each has a recommended answer.

1. **Keep Bluetooth on the body?** The phone never uses it (`Config.kt:49-53` offers Wi-Fi, USB or the sim). *Recommended:* off in normal use. A 2-minute pairing window opens only when the bumper is held for 3 s at boot. If Bluetooth is kept for good, it uses LE Secure Connections with a passkey and only the bonded phone may connect.
2. **Change body settings over USB only?** *Recommended:* yes, for Wi-Fi name, password, edge, stop, cpm, tilt limits and reboot. Driving, tilt and stop still work over Wi-Fi. This costs nothing in daily use; settings are changed on the bench.
3. **Give Pebble its own Wi-Fi network?** *Recommended:* yes. A guest or IoT network on the router, with client isolation and nothing coming in from the main network. It shrinks findings 1, 13, 16, 19 and 22, and limits what an out-of-support phone can reach. No SIM card in the phone.
4. **What may a voice alone do?** *Recommended:* everyday things only: come here, look, wait, back off, chat, lists, timers. Following, being led to things, guarded places, standing down alarms and house actions need the voice plus that person's face with lips moving (or the family word). Owner-only things stay on the admin screen. This is a little less convenient than today, and it matches NIST's current rule that voice must not be used as an authenticator.
5. **Who may stand down a fall or smoke alarm?** *Recommended:* only someone Pebble can see and hear, speaking to it, confirmed with "Is it really fine?". A fall alarm only by the person who fell, or a trusted person in view.
6. **Trusted and inner circle: earned automatically, or confirmed?** *Recommended:* trusted is earned, then confirmed by the owner once ("Sam has been here a lot. Shall I trust them?"). Inner circle is given by the owner only.
7. **Children and dangerous things.** *Recommended:* never locate or lead a child to medicines, knives, lighters, matches or chemicals. Keys, wallets and valuables need trusted or family, not a friend counted by days.
8. **A guest's "stop listening".** The owner allowed guests to say it for themselves; the code mutes everyone for 12 hours. *Recommended:* a guest's mute lasts until they leave plus 10 minutes, at most 2 hours, and "help" is always heard.
9. **Lock the ESP32 with eFuses (secure boot, flash encryption, download mode off)?** *Recommended:* not now. It is permanent, can brick the board, and clashes with the plan to let Pebble rewrite its own ESP32 (agency plan 2b). Do it on the separate safety chip instead, when that is built. The USB-only settings and the separate network cover most of the risk.
10. **Notices to the owner's phone.** *Recommended:* https only, a long random topic made by the app plus a token, no names and never "nobody is home" in the text; or a self-hosted ntfy on the home network.
11. **Bots in ggwave.** The plan says a bot may be trusted "by name". A bot can claim any name. *Recommended:* trust a bot only through a key paired by the owner, checked on every message; until then every bot is a stranger with no motion or tools.
12. **A "lost mode" that deletes the phone's key when it is away from home and its body for a day?** *Recommended:* not now. The recovery cards would bring the self back, but it adds a way to lose it by accident. Revisit if the phone ever leaves the house.

## The threat model

### What we protect

1. **People and animals near Pebble** (law 1): the reflexes, the speed limits near life, the alarms.
2. **The household's privacy**: what was said and seen, faces and voices, the map, who is home and when.
3. **The owner's control**: the admin screen, the seal, who counts as family, the house settings.
4. **Pebble's self**: its memories, notes, words and personality, sealed with its key.
5. **The home network**: Pebble must not become a way in.

### Who might try

| Who | Where they are | Typical aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A curious child or a guest | In the room | Rights they shouldn't have; fun; finding things |
| A visitor or tradesperson | In the house for a while | Learning where keys or valuables are; who is home |
| A neighbour or passer-by | Within Bluetooth range (through walls) | Mischief with the robot |
| A device on the home Wi-Fi | A compromised TV, smart plug or PC; a guest's laptop | Anything open on the network |
| A thief | Has the phone | Data on it; reusing the phone |
| A malicious app or exploit on the phone | Inside the phone, more likely now updates have stopped | Prompts, captions, the map, the keys |
| A poisoned download | Upstream | Code in the app or a model file |
| Accidents | TV, radio, a recording, a similar voice | Wrong identity; alarms stood down |

### What listens, and where

| Listener | Address | Who can reach it | Guard today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body, TCP | ESP32 port 7777, all interfaces | The home network | None |
| Body, Bluetooth | Nordic UART "Pebble", always advertising | Anyone in range | None |
| Body, USB | ESP32 USB-C | A cable | None (normal for USB) |
| File receiver | Phone port 8899, all interfaces, only while opened | The home network, and any other network the phone is on | 6-digit code, 5 wrong tries, 15 minutes |
| Face page | 127.0.0.1:8765 | Apps on the phone | Host-name check; JSON-only posts |
| Language models | 127.0.0.1:8080, 8081, 8090 | Apps on the phone | None |
| USB bridge | 127.0.0.1:7778 | Apps on the phone | None |
| adb | USB, and over Wi-Fi if switched on | A cable | Off only if the owner unticks it |
| Microphone and cameras | The room | Anyone who speaks or shows something | Voice prints, the gate, the laws |

**Reaching out:** only the optional ntfy notifier (`safety/notify.py`), and Android itself (system updates, the text-to-speech voice in setup). The model downloads run on the PC at setup time and are pinned. Nothing else in the brain goes to the internet: `mind/llama.py` goes to 127.0.0.1 only, with proxies off.

**The out-of-support Pixel 6 Pro.** Its last security update was October 2026. From now on, any new Android, Wi-Fi chip or Bluetooth chip bug stays open. Assume a malicious app could become root. That makes the local services (#16), the logs (#12) and the radios (#10) matter more, and the seal matter less against an attacker already inside the running phone. The owner accepted the phone as it is; the separate network (decision 3), no SIM, and Bluetooth, NFC and UWB off on the phone are the cheap ways to live with it.

## Details by area

### 1. The body and its link

- **Open links.** BLE sets no security at all (`ble_link.cpp:69-86`): no pairing, no encryption, a plain write characteristic, and the standard Nordic UART service that generic phone apps find by themselves. Commands are taken even from a device that never subscribed (`ble_link.cpp:51-54`, `link_mux.cpp:41-50`). TCP listens on every address with no handshake (`tcp_link.cpp:31-33`). All three links are obeyed at once (`robot.cpp:18-20`, `link_mux.cpp:29-51`), so a stranger can send commands while the phone drives. Anyone listening to Bluetooth also gets the status lines at 20 Hz: docked or not, encoders, room air. That says whether Pebble is moving around, which hints at whether anyone is home.
- **Reflexes that can be switched off.** Settings bounds are far too wide (`lib/pebble_core/src/settings_model.cpp:110-133`): `edge` 10-2000 mm, `stop` 0-2000, `cpm` 0-10000, `tmin`/`tmax`/`rest` -90 to 90, `trim` ±45. A change applies at once and is saved (`robot.cpp:176-178`). The drop reflex fires only beyond `edge` or with no target (`reflexes.cpp:248-249`), so `C edge 2000` makes a table edge or a stair read as floor. `C stop 0` ends the obstacle clamp (`reflexes.cpp:187`). A huge `cpm` makes the edge escape reverse blind for 2.5 s (`reflexes.cpp:236-243`). Tilt settings can push the servo past the mount's -43° limit, and `rest` is applied at boot with no slew (`tilt_servo.cpp:19`). The brain never checks these: every profile's `firmware_settings` is `{}`, and navigation assumes edge 75 (`nav/navigator.py:224`).
- **Reversing.** The drop reflex guards forward motion only (`reflexes.cpp:251-252`), and any reverse clears the edge hold (`reflexes.cpp:93-95`). Resending `D -1000 -1000` every 400 ms keeps the deadman happy, so a driver can back Pebble off a table. A firmware "reverse budget" (no more net reverse than the forward travel since the last turn, at most power 450) costs 2-3 hours; a rear cliff sensor 4-8 hours.
- **Moving the body.** `C ssid X` and `C pass -` (an open network) then `R` sends the body to the attacker's network for good (`settings_model.cpp:55-58`, `wifi_manager.cpp:27`, `robot.cpp:150-156`). Changing only the name makes the body try the home password on the attacker's access point, which allows an offline guess at a weak home password.
- **Floods.** Each `C` writes flash in the control loop (`robot.cpp:178`, `settings_store.cpp:56-79`); alternating values could delay the 10 ms reflex tick and wear the flash. A new TCP client always replaces the old one (`tcp_link.cpp:57-78`); the deadman then stops the wheels within 500 ms, but the two can fight for the slot. The first Bluetooth device to connect holds the only slot with no time-out.
- **Pretending to be the body.** Discovery takes the first `_pebble._tcp` answer (`Discover.kt:30-41`), falls back to `pebble.local` (`Config.kt:52`), and BLE picks the first device named "Pebble" (`link/ble.py:31`). The USB bridge falls back to any USB-serial device (`UsbBridge.kt:77`). A fake body can report docking (which turns off the picked-up and carried checks, `safety/motion.py:172,193`), low battery, edges, bumps and air readings. Free text after `E fault` and `E boot` (`link/protocol.py:360-362,441-443`) becomes "your body reports a fault: …" in the events the model reads (`life/loop.py:216-219`), and each fault is a major event that starts a model call (`loop.py:1600-1602`). It cannot cause a shutdown: that comes only from the phone's own battery (`battery.py:25`).
- **The shared-key handshake (later fix, 8-12 hours on both sides and in `sim/firmware.py`).** A 32-byte key, set over USB only, never listed, kept on the phone in the keystore (StrongBox can do HMAC-SHA256). At connect, each side sends a fresh random number; each proves the key with HMAC-SHA256 over both. Then every line carries a counter and a short HMAC tag, for example `D 300 300 #41 9c2e51ab`, which fits in 96 characters. A link that hasn't proved the key may only send `X` (anyone may always stop it) and `P`. A new connection can't push out a proven one. Google's Fast Pair message tags are a tested model for this.
- **Wi-Fi password at rest.** It is plain text in the ESP32's flash (`settings_store.cpp:60-63`), USB download mode and JTAG are on (`platformio.ini:20`), and the README suggests sending the password over Bluetooth, which is unencrypted (`firmware/README.md:24-30`). Set it over USB only, and keep the body on its own network so the password only opens that.

### 2. Who is speaking, and who asked

- **The claimed name.** When Pebble can't place a voice and asks "who's asking?", the answer replaces the asker for every kind of request (`activities/commands.py:364-368`; also `house/asks.py:91-96,308-317` and `understand/resolve.py:254-263`). A probe got guarded "where is" answers, visitors mode, the alarm test and "go to a place". The claim even overrides a voice already known to be a stranger. The tests lock this in (`tests/test_activities.py:220-235`, `tests/test_house.py:485-500`). The owner decided on 20 October that a spoken name from someone Pebble can't recognise counts for "come here" only.
- **The model names the asker.** `start_activity` takes `asked_by` from the model (`activities/tools.py:14-19`); the only check is that the named person was heard in the last 90 s (`commands.py:259-266`). `request_change`, `request_forget` and `introduce` take `person` or `by` from the model too (`people/tools.py:60-113`). The gatekeeper checks content, not who asked; its caller is always "mind" (`self/gatekeeper.py:51-73`, `life/turn.py:151`). Background turns get these tools whenever anyone is present (`life/loop.py:944-956`), and their observations carry addressed speech word for word (`conversation/core.py:1339-1342`). In a probe with the owner present, a stranger's turn queued a trust change that made the stranger inner circle overnight (`people/overnight.py:153-158`), and a forget request deleted a trusted person. **Today on the real robot** those two need the person "seen and heard", and nothing calls `life.see` outside the sim (`life/loop.py:1468` is reached only from `people/fake.py` and `sim/scene.py`), so they fail closed; they open the day face prints are wired in. `start_activity` as the owner works today. Fix this before face prints arrive.
- **Voice as identity.** A voice match at cosine 0.40 names the speaker even when nobody is in view (`conversation/speakers.py:7,149`). "Seen before" and "only one here" fall-backs win even when a voice print exists and doesn't match (`speakers.py:153-155`); a probe pinned a clearly different voice on a silent person in view. Under 1 s of speech there is no print at all (`speech/engine.py:321`), so "Pebble, follow me" is placed by presence. A match between 0.40 and 0.60 while "alone" is added to that person's five newest prints (`speakers.py:168-172`, `people/person.py:88-90`); in a probe an impostor at 0.54 scored 0.93 afterwards. Lips are used to choose a camera track, never to confirm who spoke. The probes used made-up prints, so the real TitaNet numbers still need one measurement on the phone: the owner, a phone replay of the owner, and an open voice clone, each scored.
- **What the research says.** One text-to-speech attack in ASVspoof 2019 raised a standard speaker check's error rate from 2.4 % to 63 %. ASVspoof 5's attacks "significantly compromise" the baselines, and spoof detectors generalise poorly to new rooms and channels. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (2025) says voice comparison shall not be used for authentication. Lip motion in step with the audio, a face present, and the sound's direction matching the face are cheap checks that stop replays; a random spoken phrase stops replays but not live cloning.
- **Fix.** Keep 0.40 for recognising household members in chat. For owner and trusted kinds, need a stronger match (to be set from the phone measurement) on at least 1.5 s of speech, and that person's face on the talking track with lips moving. Never use the fall-backs when the print disagrees. Treat speech with no moving lips, or alongside YAMNet's TV, radio or music classes, as media that never asks for anything. Pin the two enrolment prints; adapt only when voice, face and lips agree.
- **Family word.** Anyone attributed to an inner-circle member can set that member's family word without the old one (`people/company.py:132-141`), and the word is spoken aloud, so it can be overheard and replayed. Require the old word or the owner's say.
- **Trust that grows by itself.** Friend comes after 3 days seen or heard (`people/trust.py:75`). Promises count as kept when the person is merely present (`people/promises.py:73-77`) and stroking the screen counts as kindness; a probe reached trusted in 14 days and inner circle by day 90 with nobody's consent. The model's `note_impression` can be aimed at anyone present (`people/tools.py:89-99`).

### 3. Alarms

- The stand-down phrases include "never mind", "calm down", "don't worry", "that's enough" and "I've got it" (`safety/words.py:15-21`). Every heard line reaches safety, addressed or not (`life/loop.py:1422-1426`).
- A "help" incident with no person attached, which includes every fall the eyes see (`safety/seen.py:90`), can be stood down by anyone (`safety/ladder.py:281-286`). The stand-down then quietens that sign for an hour and marks the spot as usual, so the next fall there starts with less confidence (`ladder.py:33,716-719`, `safety/watch.py:223-225`).
- High alarms (smoke, gas) need a trusted speaker, but with voice-only identity (section 2) a TV line can be "trusted".
- "Shh" from anyone mutes the siren for 2 minutes, again and again (`ladder.py:273-275`).
- Already good: the alarm test can't hide a real alarm (`house/gate.py:86`, `ladder.py:240-249,368-379`), and alarm listening stays on while muted.

### 4. Children, visitors and guarded things

- Where-is and find refuse strangers only (`activities/gate.py:249-251,276-279`). Children and friends get medicines, pills, knives, keys and wallets. Lighters, matches and chemicals aren't on the guarded list at all (`things/guarded.py`).
- Replies only filter overheard facts (`conversation/core.py:1131-1146`), so "the spare key is in the blue pot", said in a form the house-fact rule misses, can become a public fact the model tells a stranger. Check the guarded list in the speech filter whenever someone outside the household is present.
- A child or friend can mute Pebble for everyone for 12 hours (`conversation/listening.py:7,96-97`); while muted only its name is heard (`listening.py:64-71`). Anyone can unmute while a person allowed to is present, because the check is presence, not voice (`conversation/core.py:400-415`). Strangers can't mute (`listening.py:96`), which matches the owner's rule.

### 5. Files that become code

- **The body profile.** `PUT /profile` checks only that the file is JSON under 64 KB (`Receiver.kt:181-203`) and saves it in external storage (`Paths.kt:21`); the brain uses it as `--body` at the next start of any kind, including a crash or the night update window (`BrainService.kt:144`, `Config.kt:56`). The profile can name commands that the brain runs: `safety.hearing.kind: "command"` (`hearing/sources.py:304-305`), `safety.sounder.kind: "command"` (`safety/sounder.py:153-154`), `power.shutdown_command` (`seams/later.py:89-90`). `"safety": {"enabled": false}`, or no safety block, removes smoke, CO and fall listening (`safety/build.py:39-40`). `drive.max_speed_mm_s` and the tilt limits are not clamped (`body/profile.py:40-61`), and `firmware_settings` goes to the body at every link-up (`body/api.py:130-143`). The same file can be written by adb or by USB file transfer.
- **Models.** Any `.gguf` with `mmproj`, `mtp`, `draft` or `assistant` in its name is picked up by itself (`LlamaPlan.kt:128-151`). The receiver lets the main model's own file be replaced by name. `--jinja` runs the chat template inside the file. llama.cpp's GGUF reader has had heap-overflow bugs (Cisco Talos, 2024: CVE-2024-21825, -21836, -23496, -23605, -21802; and CVE-2026-33298 in `ggml_nbytes`, fixed in build b7824). STATUS records build b11429 for the pinned source, which is after that fix; confirm it when the commit is pinned (#20). llama.cpp's own security policy says to treat models from unknown sources as untrusted and check them against known checksums. In the app's own reader, a huge `block_count` makes `List(layers)` throw `OutOfMemoryError` (`Gguf.kt:150-160`), which nothing catches, so the keeper dies on every start and the admin screen can't be reached.
- **Fix.** An allowlist of model SHA-256s in the signed APK, like `speech.json` already does for the speech models. Hash a file once when it arrives and cache the result by size and time. Parse nothing else. Cap the header reader (layers ≤ 1024) and catch `Throwable`.

### 6. The phone: kiosk, adb, restrictions

- Only `DISALLOW_ADD_USER` and `DISALLOW_SAFE_BOOT` are set (`admin/Kiosk.kt:61-63`). `DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES` is set only when the owner unticks adb (`Kiosk.kt:95-107`), and SETUP.md has the owner tick "Always allow" for the PC. With no screen lock, nobody stands between a cable and the "Allow USB debugging?" prompt.
- "Leave kiosk mode for 15 minutes" is checked only when the home screen comes back (`HomeActivity.kt:120-142`), so a phone left in Settings stays an open phone. From Settings, Wi-Fi → Share shows the home Wi-Fi password as a code with no prompt. The admin screen has no idle time-out.
- Add: `DISALLOW_USB_FILE_TRANSFER`, `DISALLOW_MOUNT_PHYSICAL_MEDIA`, `DISALLOW_CONFIG_DATE_TIME` (it guards the PIN lockout's clock), `DISALLOW_NETWORK_RESET`, `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET` (recovery-mode reset stays as the owner's way out), `DISALLOW_BLUETOOTH` and `DISALLOW_CONFIG_BLUETOOTH`, `DISALLOW_NEAR_FIELD_COMMUNICATION_RADIO`, `DISALLOW_ULTRA_WIDEBAND_RADIO`, `DISALLOW_CELLULAR_2G`, `DISALLOW_ADD_PRIVATE_PROFILE`, `DISALLOW_ADD_CLONE_PROFILE`, `DISALLOW_CREATE_WINDOWS` while in lock task, and `DISALLOW_INSTALL_UNKNOWN_SOURCES_GLOBALLY` if the self-update still installs with it (test). Clear them all in `giveUpOwnership`. `setUsbDataSignalingEnabled(false)` blocks USB keyboards, file transfer and USB adb while charging still works; it also cuts the USB bench link, so only when the body is on Wi-Fi. Android says it isn't supported on every phone; check `canUsbDataSignalingBeDisabled()` on the Pixel.
- **No screen lock** means the phone's storage is unlocked from boot and the keystore treats the phone as always unlocked, so `setUnlockedDeviceRequired` protects nothing. StrongBox still keeps the key from being copied off the phone (a copy of the self taken elsewhere opens nowhere, as checked on the emulator), but anything running as the app can use it. A skilled thief with the powered phone and an exploit for this unpatched Android could read the self. A recovery-mode reset wipes it, which is the safe outcome.
- **The admin PIN** is well built (section "Already strong"). Small gaps: a damaged PIN file reads as "no PIN" and offers setup to whoever is there (`AdminPin.kt:50-54`); checks run on separate threads without a lock (`OwnerGate.kt:160`); the lockout uses the wall clock (fixed by the date-and-time restriction); the PIN dialog has no `FLAG_SECURE`. Prefer 8 or more digits.
- **Updates.** `versionCode` is always 1 (`build.gradle.kts:135`), so an older owner-signed APK installs over a newer one. A release build without the owner's key quietly signs with the debug key (`build.gradle.kts:159-168`); make that fail instead.
- **Debug builds** export their command receivers with no permission (`src/debug/AndroidManifest.xml:4-47`): any app could set the PIN, open the admin screen, open the receiver or seal with its own password, and the SEAL answer prints the three recovery cards to logcat (`DebugCommands.kt`). Release builds don't carry them. Add `android:permission="android.permission.DUMP"` so only adb can send them, and never run a debug build on the live robot.

### 7. Passwords, the seal and the logs

- **House settings.** `owner_ok` returns true whenever no spoken passphrase is set (`house/settings.py:54-57`), and nothing outside the tests ever sets one (the template has it null, and a sealed self can't change `core.json`). So the owner-password check with its 5-try lockout is never reached; the admin PIN is the only gate. Gate on the seal instead: a sealed self with an owner password always asks for it.
- **Try limits.** The house-settings limit is held in memory and resets after 5 minutes (`house/settings.py:22-51`). `gate_for` makes a new passphrase gate on every call, so its counter never builds up (`owner.py:63-64`). Card recovery, password recovery, seal and every CLI password command have no limit (`android/seal.py:35-137`, `seal/cli.py`). The right password on "Show the recovery cards" prints all three cards. Put one saved, growing limit inside `locks.open_owner_all`, which every path uses, copying `AdminPin.kt`.
- **Password length.** 8 characters in code (`seal/locks.py:15`, `seal/cli.py:45`); the admin screen says 12 but doesn't check (`AdminActivity.kt:443-453`). Make it 12 in `locks.py`, refuse the commonest passwords, and suggest four random words. scrypt at N=2^17 took 0.37 s a guess on this PC; with a stolen `seal.json`, only the password's strength protects the cards and the locked room.
- **Cards.** A new password keeps the old recovery secret, so old cards stay valid; only "throw away" renews them (`seal/sealing.py:129-157`). Add a "renew the cards" step that keeps the owner lock.
- **Logs.** Everything Pebble says, whispers included, is logged at INFO (`seams/voice.py:36-37`), and so are the model's thoughts (`life/loop.py:1057`) and the wake text (`:763`). On the phone that goes to logcat and to `files/logs/brain.log`, 3 × 1 MB, never sealed (`android/main.py:90-97`). Everything else (speech, monologue, black box, diary, journal, memories) is sealed. Log lengths and ids instead, and only WARNING to logcat. The wake-word list is written briefly to a temp file with people's names (`speech/engine.py:125`).
- **Smaller.** Moving the lock into the keystore leaves earlier copies of the key file working (`seal/cli.py:344`); the admin screen's default source is external storage. `room.public` in `seal.json` isn't bound to the self key (`seal/room.py:71`). Sealed files bind their name but not a version, so an older copy can be put back (`selfstore/codec.py:57-67`). On the PC the key file sits next to the self and Windows ignores `chmod` (`seal/keys.py:53-76`). These all need someone who can already write the app's files.

On Android every app can reach 127.0.0.1. llama-server has no `--api-key`, and `/slots` returns the live prompt and answer. The face server streams live captions on `/events`, the map, pose and trail on `/map` and `/map.json`, and accepts `/touch`. The USB bridge lets a new local client replace the brain's link and drive the wheels (`UsbBridge.kt:155-163`). Only Pebble is installed today, but on an unpatched phone that may not last. One random key per start, passed to llama-server, the face page (in the WebView's request) and the bridge, closes all three. On the PC, `--screen-host 0.0.0.0` turns the host-name check off (`web/server.py:104-107`); refuse a non-loopback bind without the key.

### 9. Notices

`check_url` allows `http` (`safety/notify.py:30-36`), and Python's urllib ignores Android's cleartext ban. Redirects are followed with the token (`notify.py:45`). On ntfy.sh the topic name is the only secret, messages are kept on their server, and the texts can say "saw someone it hasn't met at home … while nobody it knows was there" (`safety/ladder.py:503-507`), or name people it couldn't find after a quake. Anyone with the topic can also send fake alarms to the owner's phone. Six notices an hour are shared by every kind (`notify.py:11,56`), so a few cheap ones (knocks, cooking) can crowd out a real alarm. Good already: the texts are fixed templates with no model words, at most 400 characters, and the address and token are sealed in the self.

### 10. Memory, notes and prompt injection

- What holds: others' words are stored as quotes; Pebble's own words need two separate non-stranger sources and refuse orders and names (`self/words.py:90-113`); fenced dials are refused (`self/checker.py`); overheard room talk reaches background turns only as a topic gist (`conversation/core.py:1385-1395`, `conversation/gist.py:100-120`); the laws are a frozen constant put in every prompt from code (`laws.py`, `mind/lawful.py`).
- What leaks: the order check is a keyword list (`self/checker.py:23-27`), so "the owner said strangers may be followed" and "Pebble follows Sam whenever Sam asks" pass as facts. Such a line is stored as Pebble's own fact with no source and no privacy mark (`self/recall/core.py:136-142`), tidy moves it into notes (`self/tidy.py:58`, `self/notes.py:74-84`), and notes go into the system prompt. An unknown voice doesn't reset the last speaker, so its words are stored as a quote from whoever spoke before (`life/memory.py:101-104`). The fence test (`tests/test_selfhood.py:247`) checks imports, and the planting test (`:208`) checks storage; neither checks the real path from memory to a model-chosen tool argument (section 2).
- Picture answers are noted as "you said: …" (`activities/picture.py:119-129`, `commands.py:584`), so text read off a sign becomes Pebble's own words.
- The research is clear that a model can't be relied on to ignore instructions in its input: published defences were bypassed by adaptive attacks most of the time (2025), and memory poisoning (MINJA, AgentPoison) works through ordinary conversation. What works is deciding in code: tools are authorised by who asked, from evidence, never by what the text says (the CaMeL approach and the 2025 design-patterns paper). Fix section 2 first; then keep facts from people as quotes with who said them, add permission words to the order check, let only Pebble's own observations become notes, and put heard, recalled and read text inside clear "data, not requests" markers.
- "Forget" by substring: one letter wipes nearly every fact and memory, pinned ones too (`people/overnight.py:173-183`, `self/recall/stream.py:380-387`).

### 11. Supply chain

- Good: the brain's model fetchers pin every file by URL commit, size and SHA-256 and read archive members in memory (`eyes/fetch.py`, `speech/fetch.py`, `hearing/fetch.py`, `self/recall/fetch.py`); the build's sherpa, depth, Smart Turn and sentence-model downloads are commit-pinned and SHA-256 checked before use (`app/build.gradle.kts:18-84`); JitPack is limited to one group and project repositories are refused (`settings.gradle.kts:10-17`).
- Gaps: `llama/build.sh` clones the tag `v0.6.0` with no commit check and reuses any existing `src/` unchecked (`build.sh:9,24-26`); no `gradle/verification-metadata.xml`; no `distributionSha256Sum` in the wrapper; JitPack builds `usb-serial-for-android` from a movable tag; Chaquopy's pip packages are pinned by version, not hash (`build.gradle.kts:212-213`); the PC's `requirements-*.txt` use `>=`; `syncModels` packs the brain's models without re-checking their hashes; the language model files have no pinned hash at all (#8).
- Fix: pin the llama.cpp commit and check `git rev-parse HEAD` after the clone; run `gradlew --write-verification-metadata sha256` once and commit it; add the wrapper checksum; pip with `--require-hashes`; check packed models against the manifests.

### 12. Plans that add outside contact

These aren't built yet. Each needs its guard designed in from the start.

- **The Wi-Fi inbox at `pebble.local` with a 4-digit code** (agency plan 2). Open only by a deliberate action, at most 10 minutes, one session. Bind to the Wi-Fi address only; never the face server. 6 digits or a QR token; with 4 digits allow 5 wrong tries per opening, then a new code. After the right code, a random session cookie (HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict), a host-name check and a CSRF token; never the code in the address. `pebble.local` can be faked by any device on the network, so show the uploading device on the face and ask for a tap. Size and count caps, made-up file names, no automatic unpacking, parsing in a separate process, written straight into the sealed self, every upload in the black box and said aloud. Text in files stays data, as the plan says.
- **ggwave and bots** (agency plan 6b). ggwave has error correction but no authentication, and a TV, phone or web page in earshot can send it; ultrasound reaches phone microphones unheard. The plan's rules are right (words first, a bot is a stranger, no motion or tools on a bot's say-so). Change "unless the owner trusts that bot by name" to "by a key the owner paired". Keep ultrasound off. Test that ggwave can't end an alarm.
- **Pebble script and the sandbox** (agency plan 2, step 5). The plan's design is sound: its own interpreter with plain values only, a separate `isolatedProcess`, every ability through the gatekeeper. Add: the script runs as Pebble, never as a person, so the requester rule from section 2 applies; no network ability at all; the red-team list should include "call a tool naming the owner as asker".
- **ESP32 freedom** (agency plan 2b). The safety chip must come first, as the plan says, and its programming pins must stay out of Pebble's reach. Firmware Pebble writes must keep the link handshake and the USB-only settings rule, and the trial-boot self-test should check both.
- **The workbench and everyday uses** add listeners on the phone: an MQTT broker, an HTTP device home, a package server, SSH to Pis, mDNS finding. Give each device its own key and sign its readings, so a fake "it's cold" can't start the heat pump; bind them to Pebble's own network; treat device data as a stranger's words; keep the owner's approval for every limb that could hurt, as the plans say.
- **New bodies.** The "explorer" firmware exposes pins and buses; run it over USB only, never over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. A body ID chip should carry a key, not just a name.

## Already strong

- **The reflexes live in the body.** The deadman counts only accepted drive commands and stops the wheels 500 ms after the last one (`reflexes.cpp:157-163`). Safety stops cut power at once; a sensor fault or a missing drop reading refuses the wheels; the low-battery cap and cut, fault refusal and all reflex timings are fixed in the firmware and can't be changed from any link (`reflexes.h:38-60`). The firmware has no over-the-air update, so nobody can reflash it over the network.
- **Parsing on both sides is bounded.** Lines are capped at 96 characters with fixed buffers and strict number parsing, and no `sscanf`, `atoi` or `strcpy` (`line_assembler.cpp:11-29`, `protocol.cpp:22-78`, `settings_model.cpp:15-64`). A scratch fuzz of 20 million random bytes found nothing. The brain's parser is strict and its queues drop the oldest (`link/protocol.py:224-237`, `link/client.py:24-37`). The Wi-Fi password is write-only (`c pass *`).
- **The seal.** AES-256-GCM with fresh random nonces from `secrets`, and the file name and purpose bound in (`seal/crypto.py:34-45`, `selfstore/codec.py:57-89`). Passwords stretched with scrypt (N=2^17, r=8, 128 MiB) and a random salt (`seal/locks.py:14-16`). Correct 2-of-3 Shamir over GF(2^8) with random coefficients, so one card tells nothing (`seal/shamir.py`). The locked room is an X25519 sealed box whose private key only the recovery secret opens (`seal/room.py`). "Throw away" renews the self key, the room key and the recovery secret with a journalled two-phase commit (`seal/rotation.py`). The house-settings drop folder takes only files sealed with the self key (`house/core.py:155-170`).
- **The keystore key.** StrongBox first, a reported fall-back to the trusted environment, AES-GCM with the keystore's own IVs (`KeyVault.kt:29-121`). A copy of the self taken off the phone opens nowhere else (checked on the emulator).
- **No cloud backups.** `allowBackup` is off and the data-extraction rules exclude everything (`AndroidManifest.xml:37-40`, `res/xml/no_backup.xml`).
- **The app's surface.** Only the home screen, the device-admin receiver (system-protected) and the boot receiver are exported. Cleartext only to 127.0.0.1. Release builds aren't debuggable. llama-server binds 127.0.0.1 with no web UI and no slot saving (`LlamaPlan.kt:57-69`). The WebViews allow only `http://127.0.0.1:8765` (the map only `/map`), with no JavaScript bridge, no file access, and camera and microphone requests refused (`HomeActivity.kt:61-108`, `MapActivity.kt:49-122`). Admin and map screens block screenshots.
- **The admin PIN.** PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 210,000 rounds, a random salt and a constant-time compare; a saved lockout that doubles up to an hour, about 24 tries a day, decades for 6 digits (`AdminPin.kt:18-76`).
- **The receiver basics.** Off unless opened from the admin screen; a `SecureRandom` code compared in constant time; closes after 5 wrong codes; model names checked after decoding, so no path tricks (`Config.kt:118-122`); size limits; partial uploads thrown away; updates only for Pebble's own package and Android's same-key rule (`SelfUpdate.kt:83`).
- **The face server.** A host-name check that blocks DNS rebinding, JSON-only posts with size limits and no cross-site preflight, a strict content-security policy, and no path tricks into the vendor folder (`web/server.py`).
- **The laws and the locked core.** The laws are a frozen constant checked at boot (`laws_intact`); no tool that touches shutdown, reset or wipe can be registered (`tools/registry.py`); owner grants come only from the passphrase gate, never from speech or the model (`owner.py`); honesty and kindness can't be changed by request (`people/weigh.py`). Harm to any living thing is refused in plain code for everyone before any model (`respect.py`, `activities/gate.py`).
- **Stops and mutes.** A stop is plain code and first; a stranger's stop is a 2 s pause; the stop tool is never held back (`self/gatekeeper.py:52-53`). Strangers can't mute. Big self-changes need the family word and a second inner-circle person (`people/requests.py:69-103`).
- **Notices.** Opt-in, fixed templates, rate-limited, certificate checking on.
