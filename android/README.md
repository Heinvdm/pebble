# Pebble on Android

The Android app that turns a phone into Pebble: it runs the Python brain from `../brain/pebble` unchanged (through Chaquopy), the language models (llama.cpp's `llama-server`), the eyes, the voice, the ears and the link to the ESP32 body, as the phone's only app. Setting up a phone: [SETUP.md](SETUP.md).

The brain stays portable. Everything Android-specific is in this folder (Kotlin) and in a few Python modules chosen only by the `android` body profile: `pebble/android/` (bridge, sensors, voice, ears, power, sound tap, seal operations, the entry point), `pebble/eyes/android.py` (AndroidEyes), the `android-app` key holder in `pebble/seal/keys.py`, and `pebble/profiles/body_android.json`. On a PC or on Linux nothing changes.

## How it fits together

```
main process (nz.pebble.robot)                         ":brain" process
  HomeActivity  home screen, kiosk, full-screen          BrainService (foreground: camera, microphone, special use)
                WebView on http://127.0.0.1:8765           Chaquopy Python 3.13: python -m pebble --body android ...
  AdminActivity behind the PIN (or a fingerprint)          Bridge, the object Python calls:
  KeeperService (foreground: connected device,               KeyVault     Android Keystore, AES-GCM, StrongBox
                 special use)                                PhoneSensors battery, skin temperature, light, proximity,
    Watchdog    heartbeat file, exit codes, restarts                      pressure, motion; batched sample rings
    LlamaServer x2  libllama-server.so on :8080 / :8081      Speech       TextToSpeech part by part; one shared microphone
    UsbBridge   ESP32 USB CDC <-> 127.0.0.1:7778                          feed: sherpa-onnx words, sound tap, YAMNet,
                                                                          the high band (48 kHz only)
    Receiver    code-guarded upload of models and updates    Alarm        siren and alarm-volume speech
  BootReceiver, PebbleAdmin (device owner)                   Eyes         CameraX, ONNX Runtime, OpenCV, a close look
                                                             Torch        the rear camera's light
  MapActivity   the map, behind the PIN                      Power        "rest": screen off, models stopped
                                                             Sentences    memory search's numbers, on one little core

":ears" process
  EarsService   bound by the brain's Speech; sherpa-onnx (VAD, recognizer, name spotter, voice prints)
                takes the microphone's samples and sends speech events back (Messenger)

":script" processes, isolated, one per running program
  ScriptService bound by the brain's ScriptLink; the Kotlin evaluator for Pebble script, JSON lines (Messenger)
```

- The speech models run in a third process, ":ears", because sherpa-onnx ends the whole process (`exit()`) when a model does not suit it: there it can only end the ears, never the brain. The microphone stays in the brain's process; its samples go to the ears over a Messenger and the events come back the same way, about 50 small messages a second.
- **When the ears end** (`EarsLink`), the brain starts them again itself, 1 s later, then after 2, 5, 10 and 30 s if they keep ending; the wait goes back to 1 s once they have run for 2 minutes. Ears that stop answering for 20 s, or stop hearing for 20 s while sound waits for them, are ended and started the same way. The new ears get the same settings. The fifth end in 10 minutes hands listening to Android's own recognizer for 30 minutes (see Safe loading).
- **What the brain hears meanwhile.** Nothing said is lost: the brain keeps the last 12 s of sound and sends it to the new ears first, with its real times, and the ears keep sound while their models load (up to 10 s), so the brain hears it a few seconds late instead of not at all. A sentence cut off by the crash is closed at once with a `voice_end` marked `"lost": true` and then heard again whole, from its start; so is one whose words had not come yet. Segment numbers keep counting across restarts, so a new sentence never reuses an old one's number. The same keeping of sound covers a change of mode, which reloads the models. `earsState()` says where the ears are (`"ears"`: `starting`, `loading`, `ready`, `restarting`, `resting`, or `android` while Android's recognizer stands in), and `hearingInfo()` gives the deaths, the restart wait, and how much sound was held, sent again and missed.
- Checked on the emulator with the test recording "Pebble, what can you see": ears ended 0.3 s into "what can you see" closed that sentence 18 ms later, were back 1.1 s later and ready 2.5 s after that, and the whole sentence came through 3.7 s after it was said. Frozen ears (`kill -STOP`) were ended 20 s later and were ready again 3.7 s after that, with the last 12 s of sound sent to them first. Ending them four more times waited 2, 5 and 10 s, and the fifth end handed listening to Android's recognizer, fed from Pebble's own microphone.
- The keeper finds the brain's process by name, and also by the process number in its heartbeat file (checked against `/proc/<pid>/cmdline`): Android leaves a process it has marked "not responding" out of its list, which made the keeper think a running brain was gone. That happened on the emulator after a debug command held a broadcast for over a minute; the debug commands now let go of their broadcast at once.
- The brain runs in its own process so the watchdog can kill and restart it without touching the face, the models or the USB link. The brain writes a heartbeat file every 5 s from inside its event loop; no heartbeat for 60 s (after 3 minutes' grace) and the watchdog kills the process. A dead brain is started again at once by Android (`START_STICKY`), and otherwise by the watchdog after 2, 5, 10, 30, then 60 s. Exit code 3 (battery) rests until the phone is charging; exit code 4 (a sealed self this phone cannot open) waits 10 minutes between tries instead of looping.
- Kotlin talks to Python only through `Bridge` (Python calls it) and the Python "sinks" Kotlin calls: AndroidEyes (`feed`, `slow`, `state`, `hazards_seen`), AndroidEars (`heard`), and the speech event sink the conversation code sets with `speechSink` (`event`). Pictures and sound stay in Kotlin; only numbers, words and voice prints cross, and a JPEG only when the mind asks to look (`eyesSnapshot`).
- The face page is the brain's own page, served on 127.0.0.1 only, shown in a WebView that refuses every address that is not that page (and denies camera and microphone requests from the page). Touches go to `/touch` exactly as in a browser. The WebView is hardware accelerated (logged at start). The page draws a 32-pixel-high picture (69 x 32 on the Pixel 6 Pro's 19.5:9 screen, whatever its resolution setting); it hands a frame to the canvas only when a pixel changed, and once nothing has changed for 0.25 s it looks 20 times a second instead of 60; any message from the brain (face, scene, ears, camera, look) or a touch brings it straight back. Measured on the emulator (Pebble_API35, software drawing, an awake face with nothing happening, camera off; percent of one core, the mean of 8 five-second samples, two runs each, against the same page drawing every frame): the app's process 95 to 98 % before, 13 to 17 % after; the WebView's renderer 37 % before, 10 to 12 % after; surfaceflinger 13 to 14 % before, 4 to 5 % after; the composer 16 to 17 % before, 2 to 4 % after. Idle, the app put 64 frames on the screen in 10 s.
- **Nothing stays lit in one place** (the Pixel 6 Pro's screen is OLED and on all day): every 2 minutes the whole face moves one pixel along a path 5 pixels wide and 3 high and back (about an hour for the round trip), and the camera square, the ears mark and the red link dot step round a 2 x 2 square; the text layer orbits on its own as before. Scenes and the alarm screen are short-lived and stay where they are.
- **A line on the face while Pebble can't talk** (`ui/FaceNote.kt`, a native line at the bottom of `HomeActivity`, over the face page, never touched by the brain): the keeper's `status.json` says why the main model is not answering, and the line says it in plain words: its file is not on the phone (with the exact name and folder), still being copied, too big for the memory, damaged or cut short, the build has no llama-server for this phone, the mind is loading or starting again, or the talk bench is running (which step of five, 10 to 15 minutes once, "nothing is wrong"). It goes as soon as the main model answers, never shows while paused, resting or with the scripted mind, and drifts a few pixels every 20 s. Taps go through it to the face. On the emulator each state showed its line, and the line went when the model was ready.
- The map screen (admin screen, Map) shows the brain's `/map` page in its own WebView that accepts only `/map` addresses, landscape like the face, and refuses screenshots. It says "Pebble has no map yet" while the brain has none and "The map shows while Pebble's brain is running" when it cannot reach the brain.

## What Android allows, honestly

- **Switching off.** No app can power a phone off, not even a device owner. `shutdown` (the 5 % battery routine) therefore means **rest**: Pebble's brain stops, the models stop, the screen goes off (`lockNow`), and the watchdog starts Pebble again when the phone is put on charge. `"power": {"kind": "android", "mode": "reboot"}` reboots instead (a device owner may), which helps nothing on a flat battery. The phone switches itself off at its own low-battery cut-off, as any phone does.
- **Starting at boot.** Pebble is the home app, so Android shows it after every restart, and `BOOT_COMPLETED` starts the keeper. Android 14 forbids starting a camera or microphone foreground service from `BOOT_COMPLETED`, so the brain is started once the home screen is up. If the brain ever starts while its face is not on screen and Android refuses it the camera and microphone, it runs without them and is restarted as soon as the face is on screen again.
- **No screen lock.** A device owner can hide only an insecure lock screen, and Android keeps an app's files locked after a restart until a secure lock is entered. So Pebble's phone has no screen lock; the admin PIN and kiosk mode guard it instead. The consequence: anyone who has the phone in their hands can see the face, nothing else (no status bar, no settings, no app switcher). Factory reset and the power menu stay available.
- **Background killing.** Android 12+ kills phantom processes (the llama-server children) beyond 32 system-wide, or when they use a lot of CPU while their app is in the background; the Pixel's Adaptive Battery holds back apps it thinks are rarely used. Pebble is the foreground app with two foreground services, so neither should happen, and the watchdog restarts what dies. SETUP.md also switches both off with adb, but nothing depends on that alone.
- **Silent updates.** Only because Pebble is the device owner. Without it, every update asks on the screen.
- **adb.** Pebble forbids USB debugging entirely (`DISALLOW_DEBUGGING_FEATURES`) once the admin PIN is set; the admin screen lets it in for 30 minutes at a time and the keeper shuts it again when the time is up. Debug builds never block it (their test commands need adb), so a debug build must never run on the live robot.
- **Lock-down** (`admin/Kiosk.kt`, set at every start, all lifted by "Stop being the device owner"): no USB file transfer, no physical media, no changing the date and time or resetting the network or the phone from Settings, no Bluetooth, NFC, ultra-wideband or 2G, no new users, private spaces or clones, no safe mode. A restriction this Android does not know is skipped (the emulator refuses the NFC one). The keeper ends an expired kiosk pause by bringing the face back to the front (lock task again), and the admin screen closes after 2 minutes without a touch.
- **USB data.** While the body is on Wi-Fi, adb is shut and no pairing is running, the keeper switches the USB port's data off (`setUsbDataSignalingEnabled(false)`; charging still works), on phones where `canUsbDataSignalingBeDisabled()` says it can. Whether the Pixel 6 Pro allows it is not known yet; the admin screen says. Choosing the USB cable for the body turns it back on.
- **Updates.** An update must have a higher version code than the app on the phone (`net/SelfUpdate.kt`), so an older signed build cannot be put back.

## Privacy: what leaves the phone

Nothing Pebble sees, hears or thinks. Camera pictures and microphone sound exist only in memory and are never written anywhere (the sound tap keeps the last 10 seconds in a ring buffer in memory, the link to the ears the last 12 seconds while listening, so a restart of the ears loses nothing, and the end-of-turn model the last 8 seconds while it is on; the eyes keep the newest picture and at most four held pictures for prints, all in memory). The sound model's 1,024 numbers for each second are kept for 30 seconds, only while the brain asks for them, and dropped a minute after it stops asking; they describe a sound and cannot be played back. The high band keeps numbers only (tones and band levels). The microphone probe writes only numbers (levels per band, the delay between two microphones) to `mic-probe.json`, never sound. Speech recognition runs inside the app (sherpa-onnx, with the models packed in the APK); the fallback, used only when those models are missing or failed, is Android's **on-device** recognizer (`createOnDeviceSpeechRecognizer`), and when a phone has neither, Pebble does not listen for words at all and says so. The voice picks a text-to-speech voice that does not need the internet. The models run on the phone. The face page loads nothing from the internet. Listening for words stops in quiet hours (22:00 to 07:00 by default), unless the safety code wakes it for a while (`AndroidEars.wake_for`).

What does reach the internet, outside Pebble: Android itself (system updates, Google Play services if present), the download of an offline text-to-speech voice (once, in setup), and the ESP32 link on Pebble's own Wi-Fi (no internet). The file receiver listens on the phone's Wi-Fi address only while the owner opens it, for 15 minutes, behind a 6-digit code sent in the `x-pebble-code` header (never in the address), takes one file and closes, and closes after 5 wrong codes. It is plain HTTP, so the file and the code cross the Wi-Fi unencrypted; that is acceptable only on Pebble's own network.

## The eyes

CameraX gives the front or the rear camera (one at a time; switching closes one and opens the other) as RGBA pictures, turned upright for the mounted phone. Kotlin runs the same models as `pebble/eyes`:

| Part | Model | How it runs on the phone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Faces | YuNet 2023mar (MIT) | OpenCV `FaceDetectorYN` at 320 wide, or ONNX Runtime at 640 |
| People and 80 everyday things | NanoDet-Plus 416 (Apache-2.0) | ONNX Runtime or OpenCV DNN; spread over frames by measured time, like CameraEyes |
| Body points | RTMPose-t body7 (Apache-2.0) | up to 3 people, up to 5 times a second |
| Slow lane, 2,010 words | YOLOE-26n export `objects.onnx` (AGPL-3.0) | own low-priority thread, at most once a second, only when the picture changed or Pebble moved, never while the mind is busy |
| Prints of things | DINOv2-small `prints.onnx` (Apache-2.0) | on demand: fingerprint, remember, look for, dock print |
| Dock tag | AprilTag tag36h11 id 0, 48 mm | OpenCV `ArucoDetector` + `solvePnPGeneric` (IPPE square), rear camera only |
| Who is speaking (lips) | MediaPipe face mesh `face_landmarks_detector.tflite` (Apache-2.0) | OpenCV DNN's TFLite reader, the same as the PC; only while a voice is heard (`hear_voice`), at most 2 faces of 32 px or more, 198 ms a face on the emulator |
| Fire and smoke | YOLOE export `hazards.onnx` (AGPL-3.0) | own thread every few seconds (`eyesHazardsEvery`), plus a small colour grid of each picture for the glow checks |

| Camera motion | none: OpenCV phase correlation on an 80 x 60 grey copy | every picture, under 1 ms; sent with the picture's results |
| Place prints | the print model on the whole picture and its four quarters | on demand (`place_print`) |

Raw boxes, scores, class numbers and the tag's two poses go to Python; Python does the rest with the brain's own code (labels, the tracker, faces joined to bodies, the two lanes merged, angles, tag yaw and its trust score, body points, prints). So a Sight from the phone follows exactly the same conventions as one from CameraEyes. The decoders are checked against the Python ones on the real models' outputs (`app/src/test/.../DecodeTest.kt`, fixtures made by `tools/decode_fixtures.py` from `brain/`), and the tag pose path against `Detector.tags` (`brain/tests/test_android.py`). The floor rays are checked the same way against `floor.rays` on exact and real-model depth maps (`FloorTest.kt`, fixtures made by `tools/floor_fixtures.py`); Python only cuts them where a low thing lies on the floor and keeps them for 1 s, like CameraEyes.

**Fastest backend.** At the first start the eyes time every way of running each model on a sample picture (ONNX Runtime with XNNPACK, plain CPU, NNAPI, and OpenCV DNN) and keep the fastest; the choice is remembered until the app's version code or the phone changes. "Measure the eyes again" on the admin screen forgets it, and the floor scan's choice, and any step down the language models remembered (`LlamaChoice`), so each tries its best setting again at the next start. It also clears the speech models' rest after load crashes and the name probes' count, the language models' talk bench runs again, and so does the memory search's (see Memory search). Measured on the PC's x86_64 emulator (SSE4.2 only, no AVX, so far slower than a phone; per picture, ms):

The floor scan is timed on its own (`floor-choice.json`): XNNPACK will not load the depth model (its Resize kernel refuses it), so it runs on the plain CPU: on the emulator 190 to 250 ms a picture on its own and 700 to 1000 ms while the fast lane shares the processor, plus about 15 to 30 ms for the rays, which is about one scan a second while moving.

On the emulator the whole fast lane ran at 8 to 10 pictures a second (faces about 35 ms, people about 65 to 90 ms, body points about 45 ms). The Pixel 6 Pro has not measured itself yet; the admin screen shows its numbers after the first start. NNAPI is deprecated from Android 15 and is offered only because it is measured, never assumed; on the Pixel it may hand a model to the Tensor chip's own accelerators, which is exactly what the first start's timing finds out.

The lips: YuNet's five face points go through both face finders, the face is cropped and turned level exactly as `pebble/eyes/speaking.py` does it, and only the two lip-shape numbers per face cross to Python, where the brain's own `SpeakingMeter` turns them into `SeenPerson.speaking`.

The phone uses the fp16 export of the depth model (50 MB instead of 99 MB) because it gives the same floor. Checked again on 19 October 2026 with the brain's own floor code on the PC: on the rendered rooms of `brain/tests/floor_render.py` (6 rooms, pitch 0 and 8 degrees, 384 rays) no ray moved by more than 3.5 cm against the fp32 model, no ray changed between open and blocked, and the quality scores agree to 0.005; against the true floor, fp32 is off by 16.4 cm on average and fp16 by 16.6 cm (95th percentile 63 and 62 cm); the four checks of `test_floor_scan_on_rendered_rooms` come out the same with both. On the PC's CPU both take about 100 ms a picture. `FloorTest.theHalfSizeModelGivesThePcModelsFloor` keeps this checked in the Kotlin tests: the phone's floor scan on the fp16 model's depth maps of four rendered rooms at two pitches must give the fp32 model's rays within 5 cm and its quality within 0.01 (fixtures `half.json`, made by `tools/floor_fixtures.py --half`, which needs `models/depth_anything_v2_small_fp16.onnx` from the build's `fetchDepth`). An earlier session found the emulator's 81 rays within 3.3 cm of fp32's, at the same speed (190 to 250 ms a picture). ONNX Runtime's XNNPACK refuses both exports (its Resize step), so the depth model runs on the plain CPU backend. Whether fp16 is faster or slower than fp32 on the Pixel's CPU is not measured yet.

The green "eyes on" square works as before: AndroidEyes tells the face page whenever a camera is open.

**A close look** (`eyesCloseUp(maxSide, zoom)`, a JPEG, or nothing; `eyesCloseUpInfo()` says how the last one was made). For reading across a room (household pack A17): the eyes put the analysis aside, open the rear camera for one full-quality still (CameraX `ImageCapture`, up to 4032 x 3024), zoom to `zoom` (4 by default, at most 10), focus on the middle, take the picture, and open the camera they had before. The zoom goes through the logical rear camera, which on a phone like the Pixel 6 Pro hands over to the telephoto lens by itself; the capture results say which lens took it (`"lens"`), and `"lenses"` lists every lens the rear camera exposes with its focal length and field of view. When the lens cannot reach the zoom asked, the rest is a crop from the middle (`"digital"`). The camera is closed for about 1 to 3 s (the eyes send `closed` and `open` as for any switch). With a test picture, the close look is the middle of that picture. The brain turns Pebble round first; nothing here moves it. On the emulator (one logical rear camera, no zoom): a 1280 x 960 still, cropped 4 times to 320 x 240, in 2.5 s, and the front camera back at 9.4 pictures a second.

**The torch** (`torch(on, level, seconds)`, `torchInfo()`, household pack A7). The rear camera's light, through CameraX when the rear camera is open and Android's torch call otherwise; `level` from 0 to 1 picks one of the phone's brightness steps where it has them (Android 13+). It always ends by itself: after `seconds` (10 minutes when not given, at most an hour), when the phone gets hot (the keeper's heat level), or when the brain says off. It follows the camera: opening the rear camera moves it onto the camera, closing it moves it back. `torchInfo()` says whether the phone has one, whether it is lit, by which route, the time left, why it last ended, and the problem if Android refused (for example "the rear camera is in use").

## The ears and the voice

Pebble listens all the time it is awake (not in quiet hours, unless the safety code wakes it), and everything below runs on the phone.

- **One microphone feed.** `Microphone` (AudioRecord, 16 kHz mono) is the only thing that opens the microphone. It feeds: the speech models, the **sound tap** (last 10 s, read from Python with `pebble.android.sounds.SoundTap`), and YAMNet sound scores (`soundScores`, for pebble-safety's alarm and event detection). Its source is `VOICE_RECOGNITION` by default (no automatic gain, no noise suppression, which keeps alarm beeps intact); `speechConfig({"mic_source": "communication"})` switches to `VOICE_COMMUNICATION`.
- **48 kHz, brought down** (`plans/better-hearing.md` C). `speechConfig({"mic_rate": 48000, "mic_channels": 1 or 2, "mic_speech_channel": 0 or 1})` opens the one AudioRecord at 48 kHz (32 and 96 kHz work too). A low-pass filter (241 taps, Kaiser window, cut at 8 kHz) and every third sample (`Decimate.kt`) give every listener above the same 16 kHz mono feed as before, taken from one channel, never the sum; the 48 kHz sound goes only to the new wide listeners (`WideSink`). If the phone refuses the wider feed, the microphone opens at 16 kHz and `micPlan()` says why (`step_down`). It stays at 16 kHz until the brain asks; the probe below suggests when. What it was checked against: sound folding back from 8.5 to 24 kHz comes out at least 60 dB down and 0 to 7 kHz passes within 0.05 dB (`DecimateTest`, with the Python twin's taps in `test/resources/hearing/decimate.json`, made by `tools/hearing_fixtures.py`); every alarm and chirp test of the brain (51) passes on sound raised to 48 kHz, given tones and hiss above 9 kHz, and brought down by the twin, once the filter's 2.5 ms delay is taken out (with the delay left in, `test_t4_under_speech` misses its second group, and so does the plain 16 kHz sound delayed by the same 40 samples without any filter: that test sits on the edge of where its frames fall); YAMNet's top class was the same in all 70 windows of seven recordings, the largest score change 0.09 (on "Silence" in a quiet gap) and the 1,024 numbers at least 0.998 alike; on the emulator, the test recording at 48 kHz with tones and hiss above 9 kHz gave the same words and found the name every time, 125 to 204 ms after it ended (127 to 358 ms at 16 kHz in the same session); the emulator's own microphone ran at 48 kHz in mono and in stereo. Bringing it down cost 0.23 % of one emulator core.
- **The sound model's numbers** (`soundVectors(from)`, for the household pack's taught sounds). YAMNet's `embeddings` output, 1,024 numbers per window, comes with the scores at no extra run. They are kept only while the brain asks (a call within the last minute), for the last 30 s (63 windows), and windows that overlap Pebble's own voice (and 0.5 s after it) are left out, as the brain's own reader leaves them out on the PC. The answer is shaped like `soundScores`: `{"total", "from", "rows": [[ms, "f16:<base64>"]], "now_ms", "kept", "running", "problem"}`, the 1,024 numbers as little-endian half floats, which `hearing/events.py`'s `unpack_vector` reads. Checked on the emulator through the brain's own `BridgeModel.take_vectors`: 16 rows in 12 s, 1,024 numbers each, and nothing kept a minute after it stopped asking.
- **The high band** (`HighBand.kt`; `highBand(from)`, `highBandMoving(on)`; its twin is `hearing/highband.py`). Only with a 48 or 96 kHz feed: every 1,024 samples (21 ms) a Hann-windowed FFT with bins 46.875 Hz apart, from 15 kHz to the top the probe found (at most 24 kHz). A tone is a peak 10 dB over the band's usual level around it (each bin keeps a slow average of its own level, and the median of its neighbours' averages, 3 to 12 bins away, is the reference) and sharp against its nearest neighbours, three times within a few frames; it is then followed as it drifts. Rows, like `soundScores`: a tone's `start` (after 0.25 s), `steady` (at 10 s within 100 Hz) and `end`, each with `hz`, `low_hz`, `high_hz`, `start`, `end`, `level_db`, `over_db`, `rise_db` and `shape`: `steady`, `pulsing` (with `period_s` and `regular`), `sweeping`, `beacon` (a burst under 1 s between 18 and 20 kHz) or `tone`; a `data` row for several tones hopping between 15 and 19.5 kHz (ggwave's band) with how many bins they used; and a `levels` row each second with the level of every 1 kHz band. Pebble's own sounds pause it, and while `highBandMoving(true)` the 20 kHz motor line is marked `"own"`. A jump in the whole band's level (a fan, a tap) is let settle for 0.5 s before new tones count: tones still pending when the jump is confirmed are dropped, and every bin's level follows quickly until it has settled (the twin found stray tones in 19 of 40 made-up cases of hiss starting after silence without that; `HighBandTest` now runs 40 such cases with none). Tested on made-up sound (`HighBandTest`: a steady 17.4 kHz tone, a 1 s pulse, a sweep, hopping data tones, a beacon, the motor line, a tone 10 dB over the room found within 2 s, 2 minutes of hiss and clicks with no tone, hiss starting after silence, speech and music below 8 kHz with no tone, 96 kHz). On the emulator, the 17.4 and 20 kHz tones of the test sound were found every loop at their true levels. It cost 0.48 % of one emulator core. The brain's twin (`hearing/highband.py`) is not written yet.
- **The microphone probe** (`MicProbe.kt`; `micProbe(options)`, about 4 minutes; `micPlan()` reads its last result). It plays sound, so it runs only when asked (debug `MICPROBE`, or the brain). It reads the phone's own claims (unprocessed source, near-ultrasound support, each microphone's position, channel mapping and response), then opens each source (unprocessed, voice recognition, mic, camcorder, voice communication) at 16, 48 and 96 kHz, mono, stereo and two- and three-channel index masks, and records 3 s of the room: the level in every 1 kHz band, whether a 48 kHz feed falls off a cliff at 8 kHz (a 16 kHz path underneath), whether two channels are one copied, and whether the echo canceller attaches. Meanwhile each recording still goes, brought down to 16 kHz, to every listener, so Pebble is never deaf for more than a moment. Then it clicks (a 1 ms chirp, 2 to 12 kHz) five times from the left speaker channel, the right one and the earpiece while recording two channels at 48 kHz, and finds each click's delay between the channels: real microphones hear it about half a millisecond apart, the near one first, and the order swaps between the ends; the delays give their spacing. Last, a quiet 10 to 22 kHz sweep shows where the path stops. The result suggests a plan (48 kHz only when the sweep shows the path above 15 kHz). On the emulator every setting opened (it fakes them), stereo was one channel copied, no click or sweep came back, and it suggested staying at 16 kHz.
- **The name spotter's gain** (`speechConfig({"kws_gain": true})`, off by default). The spotter hears the raw sound while the recognizer hears it scaled towards -23 dBFS; with the switch on, the spotter gets the same scaling, worked out slowly from speech only (the voice detector's speech windows, about 5 s of speech to settle) and held between, at most 30 times. Measured on the emulator with the test recording, about four names each: as loud as recorded, found 4 of 4 either way (gain 0.7); 20 dB quieter, 4 of 4 either way (gain 10); 30 dB quieter, 0 of 4 without and 4 of 4 with it (gain 30); 40 dB quieter, 0 of 4 without and 3 of 4 with it; a LibriSpeech sentence without the name, as recorded and 30 dB quieter, gave no false names either way (only about 25 s each). The quiet names came later than loud ones, with or without the gain: 370 to 610 ms after they ended with it, 122 to 450 ms without it at 20 dB quieter. The plan's switch-on test, no more false names in 30 minutes of television and meetings, needs the PC or the phone.
- **Echo.** Android's `AcousticEchoCanceler` is attached to the feed when the phone offers it (`"echo_cancel": false` takes it off). `earsState()` says `"aec": true` only when it really is on, and the conversation code lets a voice alone interrupt Pebble only then. Every sample also carries a "Pebble is speaking" flag, and every speech event carries `pebble_speaking`.
- **Words: sherpa-onnx in Kotlin** (`SherpaEars`, in the ":ears" process), with the four models pinned by the brain in `pebble/speech/models/speech.json`:
  - Silero VAD opens and closes segments (`voice_start`, `pause`, `voice_end`); 0.4 s of sound before the start goes with the segment.
  - NeMo's streaming fast-conformer transducer (480 ms chunks, int8) gives `partial` words while someone talks and a `final` at the end. The audio it hears is scaled towards -23 dBFS (at most 30 times louder), because the recognition source has no automatic gain; the final carries the `gain`, and also `level_db` and `peak_db` from the raw sound.
  - A keyword spotter (zipformer, 3.3M) listens for Pebble's name, with the keyword line the conversation code sends. When a pause follows the name by 96 ms, 0.64 s of silence is pushed through the spotter at once, so the name is heard about 0.12 s after it ends instead of about 0.5 s (`"flushed": true` on the event). The silence goes into probes, not into the spotter that hears everything: pushing it into that one changed what it heard next, and it then missed the name in a quarter to a half of the tries. Each probe is a second copy fed the same sound; after a flush it is replaced by a new one primed with the last 1.3 s of sound. The two probes (`kws_probes`, 0 to 4, default 2) sit 0.16 s apart on the spotter's 0.32 s step, because whether this model catches a name can depend on where the name falls on that step (it did with the test recording). A second find of the same word within 1.5 s is dropped. Each probe costs about as much as the spotter itself (about 1.6 % of one PC core).
  - TitaNet-S makes a 192-number voice print of every segment of 1 s or more, sent with the final or just after it (`print_after_final`).
  - Smart Turn v3.2 (BSD-2-Clause, 8.7 MB, int8; fetched at build time from a pinned commit and checked by SHA-256, packed in the APK) judges whether the person has finished. It is off until the brain asks (`speechConfig({"turn_model": true})`); then, in words mode, every `pause` and `voice_end` is followed by its own `turn_end` event (`"turn_end"`, the chance from 0 to 1 that the person has finished; `"after"`, `pause` or `voice_end`; `"turn_end_ms"`). The model runs on its own thread after the pause or the end has been sent, so it never holds them, or the final words, back. It reads the last 8 s of sound through Whisper's own features, computed in Kotlin and matching the reference extractor to within 0.00001 (`TurnFeaturesTest`). On the emulator: 0.92 to 0.98 after "Pebble" and after "what can you see", 0.06 at a pause inside the sentence, 150 to 460 ms a run (the report estimates 40 to 120 ms on one X1 core). It runs in the ears process beside sherpa-onnx's own ONNX Runtime; a missing or broken model only leaves the fields out.
  - The ears time themselves against the sound's own length, every 30 s of sound. Over half of it, they drop one name probe and remember that (`ears-choice.json`), down to none, so a slow or hot phone gives up the fast name before hearing falls behind. On the emulator in words mode, with two probes and the end-of-turn model: 0.21.
- **End of turn, one at a time.** A new `pause` drops any end-of-turn run still waiting for a pause, and a `voice_end` drops waiting runs for its own and older pauses; `voice_end` runs are never dropped, so a slow phone cannot build a backlog. `engine.turn_model` in `hearingInfo()` says `missing` when the model is not in the app, even while it is off.
- **Modes**: `words` (everything), `keywords` (the voice detector and the name spotter only, cheap, for quiet hours or when someone asked Pebble to stop listening: `voice_start`, `pause`, `voice_end` and `keyword` events, with the same flush, so the name comes just as fast; measured below) and `off`, chosen by the brain (`AndroidEars`). Changing the mode, the keywords, the VAD timings or the number of probes reloads the models (1.2 to 4.9 s on the emulator), and the sound heard meanwhile is kept and heard after (up to 10 s); the other settings apply at once.
- **Safe loading**, a second layer behind the separate process. The ears write a marker before loading the models and remove it after. If they end twice in a row while loading, Pebble listens with Android's own recognizer for an hour (then two, four, up to a day) and then tries the models again by itself; `hearingInfo()` says why and until when. If the ears end 5 times in 10 minutes for any reason, the brain also uses Android's recognizer for 30 minutes. Model files are copied out of the APK once, compared by size (or by content for small files), and files the manifest no longer names are deleted. Checked on the emulator: two deaths while loading switched Pebble to Android's recognizer with that message, and when the rest ran out the ears loaded the models and listened again.
- **The voice** speaks part by part: `sayPart(id, text, last, whisper)` queues a sentence while the mind is still writing the next one; a long first sentence is cut at its first comma so the first words come sooner. Events report when each part starts, each word, and when it is done; `stopSpeaking()` stops at once and drops what is queued (the event says how many characters were spoken). Whispers play at 35 % volume. `AndroidVoice` still writes `logs/speech.jsonl`, and the face's lip-sync is driven as before. The alarm voice and the siren (650 / 950 Hz) play on the alarm stream at full volume.
- **The fallback**, Android's on-device recognizer, is fed from Pebble's own microphone feed through `RecognizerIntent.EXTRA_AUDIO_SOURCE` (Android 13+). If a phone's recognizer refuses that three times in a row, it opens the microphone itself, and then Pebble stops listening for words while the safety code wants the sound tap (the alarm listener wins).

Measured on the emulator (x86_64 with SSE4.2 only, so several times slower than the Pixel 6 Pro), with a recording looped as the microphone:

## Memory search: the sentence model

Pebble's memory search (`brain/README.md`, The sentence model) turns every memory and every question into 384 numbers. On the phone the numbers come from Kotlin (`recall/`), from the same model by the same steps as on the PC, so numbers made on one are good on the other.

- **The model:** snowflake-arctic-embed-xs, its int8 ONNX file and word list (Apache-2.0, 23 MB), fetched by the build from the commit and SHA-256s the brain pins in `pebble/self/recall/fetch.py` (`fetchRecall`, into `models/`, which stays out of git; `-Ppebble.recallModel=off` leaves it out) and packed in the APK under `recall/`. The brain's own copy is no longer packed with the brain's Python, where it could never run.
- **The same steps as `embed.py`:** BERT's uncased word pieces (`WordPiece.kt`: control characters dropped, Chinese characters set apart, Python's lower case including its rule for a Greek sigma at the end of a word, accents stripped, punctuation split off, at most 128 pieces), `input_ids`, `attention_mask` and `token_type_ids` as int64, the first token's row of `last_hidden_state`, scaled to length 1. Questions get "Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: " in front; memories don't. The brain sees an object named `snowflake-arctic-embed-xs-int8`, the PC's name, so stored numbers stay; 384 numbers; related from 0.55 (`PhoneSentences` in `pebble/android/bridge.py`, given to the brain as `LifeParts.embedder` by `pebble/android/main.py`). The numbers cross as little-endian floats.
- **One little core, never while a language model writes:** the model runs on its own thread with one ONNX Runtime thread, at background priority, held on one core with `taskset` (checked before every run and set again if Android moved it). The core is the slowest one the language models leave free, by the same ranking llama.cpp's cores get: core 2 on the Pixel 6 Pro. Before each batch it asks llama-server's `/slots` on 8080, 8081 and 8090 (the talk bench) whether an answer is being written (a slot busy with at least one token written); reading a prompt does not count. It asks again every 0.1 s while a batch runs, and when writing starts, or a question comes, the batch stops at once (ONNX Runtime's terminate flag) and is done again later, whole. Notes go in batches of up to 32, in order. A batch waits up to 60 s for the writing to end, or for a hot phone to cool, and then gives up; the brain keeps those memories and numbers them later. A question waits at most 1.2 s for the writing to end and then gives up, and the brain searches by time and importance alone. Questions still run on a hot phone: one costs milliseconds of one little core.
- **Measured at the first start and after "Measure the eyes again"**, in the brain's process, with the eyes and ears running (it waits up to 3 minutes for both, then measures anyway and records which ran), at a quiet moment (nothing being written, not hot): loading, a memory alone, a question and a batch of 32, each timed on the model's own thread. The result goes to `recall-choice.json` in the no-backup folder, keyed by app version, phone model, ABI, model file and core, and shows on the admin screen ("Memory search: ..."). Until it has measured, the model is used, as on the Pixel 6 Pro.
- **The ladder:** the sentence model on one little core is the Pixel 6 Pro's rung. A question over 500 ms, a memory over 250 ms in a batch, a model that won't load or fails three times in a row steps down to the brain's stand-in (words hashed into 384 numbers): at once when this run has not used the model's numbers yet, otherwise from the next start, so the two kinds of numbers are never mixed. Heat never switches the model, because the stand-in's numbers can't be compared with the model's and a change of model makes the brain number every memory again; heat only holds the memories back.

Measured on the emulator (Pebble_API35, x86_64 with SSE4.2 only, 4 cores, all with the same clock, so it uses core 0): loading 0.23 to 0.45 s; through the bridge, the middle of three runs of 10 to 15 tries each: a memory alone 7 to 8 ms (single tries 6 to 16), a question 10 to 12 ms (9 to 18), a batch of 32 memories 358 to 388 ms (356 to 437), about 12 ms a memory; through the brain's own object, 32 memories in 365 to 600 ms. Its own bench, three times, with the eyes and ears running: a memory 15 to 30 ms, a question 10 to 18 ms, a batch of 32 391 to 632 ms (the slowest while the eyes were measuring themselves at the same time). Against the PC's onnxruntime (fixtures `app/src/test/resources/recall/cases.json`, made by `tools/recall_fixtures.py` from `brain/`): all 48 lines give the PC's word-piece ids (also checked in `WordPieceTest`), every line alone matches within 0.000001 in cosine, and lines in a batch within 0.0009. That is the int8 model's own wobble: it sizes its numbers over the whole batch, so on the PC the same batch moves by 0.0009 between onnxruntime's fused and plain int8 steps, and a line alone against the same line in a batch by up to 0.002. With a real llama-server (the pinned build, LFM2.5-230M) writing 900 tokens over 16 s on 8081, a question was given up after 1.4 s and a batch of 8 memories waited 14.4 s, until the writing ended, then ran. A batch of 32 long memories (2.2 s on its own) stopped when the server started writing 200 tokens in the middle of it, and was done again after the writing ended (6.1 s in all); a question asked 0.3 s into such a batch was answered in 34 ms, and the batch was done again after it (2.5 s in all). With the thermal status forced to severe, a question ran in 15 ms and a batch was given up after 60 s. After "Measure the eyes again" the choice was gone, the brain started unmeasured and measured again once the eyes and ears ran and the phone had cooled. A saved stand-in choice started the brain on the stand-in without loading the model.

## Pebble script on the phone

Pebble's own small programs (`brain/README.md`, Pebble script) run live on the phone in `ScriptService` (`script/`), a service with `android:isolatedProcess="true"`. Android gives each one a user of its own with no permissions: it can't see the app's files (the app's data folder isn't even there for it), open its preferences, reach the keystore or open any socket.

- **Why Kotlin.** The brain's evaluator is Python, but Chaquopy can't start in an isolated process: `AndroidPlatform` opens SharedPreferences, which Android refuses there, and Chaquopy unpacks Python into the app's files, which that process can't see (checked on the emulator, 23 October 2026). So `script/` holds a Kotlin port of `pebble/script/evaluate.py` (`Machine.kt`, with `Program.kt`, `Values.kt` and `Json.kt`) and of `pebble.script.host`'s JSON lines (`ScriptHost.kt`: `load`, `run`, `reply`, `stop`, `drop`, `ping`, `quit`). It runs the JSON program only and holds only plain values. It keeps every limit the Python one keeps: 100,000 steps, 50 ms of computing between ability calls, text, items, nesting, 256 KB of values, 20 calls deep, and bombs refused before they are worked out. It pauses at each ability call until the brain's reply comes back.
- **The same answers as Python.** The 139 conformance vectors in `brain/pebble/script/vectors/` are copied into the build (`syncVectors` for the JVM tests, `syncVectorAssets` for the instrumented ones; never by hand) and replay identically: `ScriptVectorsTest` on the PC's JVM, `ScriptServiceTest` on the phone's runtime, and again through the isolated service.
- **One process per running program.** The brain's `Phone` engine (`pebble/script/host.py`) calls `scriptOpen`, `scriptAsk` and `scriptClose` on the bridge. `ScriptLink`, in `:brain`, binds a new isolated instance for each program (`bindIsolatedService`, so each has its own process), refuses one whose `ping` doesn't say it is isolated, and passes the JSON lines over a Messenger. If no answer comes within the brain's wait (3 s, 10 s for a load), it unbinds, Android kills the process ("isolated not needed"), and the run counts as a crash. The service's own guard also ends it if one message keeps it computing for 5 s, or if its heap stays over 64 MB after a collection (`memory_mb`, 16 to 256); it says why first when it can.
- **Checked on the emulator** (`ScriptServiceTest`, 8 tests): the vectors on the phone's runtime and through the service; the service's own pid and an isolated uid (99000 and up); a silent program's process gone 3.0 to 3.4 s after it was asked; with a long wait, the service ending itself after 5.0 s with "it computed for too long"; the 50 ms limit stopping a loop on its own with the service still answering; and all 15 starter programs dreamed end to end from the brain's Python through `Bridge` and the isolated service, with the same calls and words as the dream in the brain's own process (0.5 to 7 s each for both dreams together, up to 15 s on a cold first run). `ScriptProbeService` (debug builds only) is the same service with a `probe` message that tries the app's files, its key file, a file the test has just written, preferences, the keystore, a local socket, the internet and a name lookup from inside the sandbox: every one is refused (`ENOENT`, `EACCES`, "missing INTERNET permission"), while the test's own process reads the same file and connects to the same local port.
- Not yet: a living Pebble running programs on the Pixel 6 Pro, and the evaluator's speed on its cores.

## The house settings (admin screen)

Around the house (`brain/README.md`, Around the house): the home town for sunrise and sunset (a New Zealand town, or a latitude and longitude), the guest Wi-Fi Pebble can show as a code, children's arrivals told to their grown-ups, the cooking watch, and whether the house is in a tsunami zone. The screen reads them from the self, sealed or not (a light read that never tidies the self, so it is safe while Pebble runs), and shows them in plain words (never the password), with how many sent changes are still waiting. While the brain runs, saving hands the changes to it without stopping it: the app's own Python (`app/src/main/python/pebble_admin/house.py`) calls the brain's `house.settings.post(self_dir, passphrase, password, changes_json, AndroidAppKeyHolder())`, which checks the owner and the values and leaves the changes (and the tsunami answer) in the self's `house-settings` folder; the running brain takes them within a few seconds, into its own copy, and the screen reads the settings again 6 s later. When the brain is not running, the held path is the fallback: the keeper holds the brain (not the language models; a hold that is not let go ends by itself after 2 minutes, and letting go never undoes the owner's pause), the app opens the self with the phone's key and calls `house.settings.change` and `setup.set_tsunami_zone`. The owner rule is the brain's `house.settings.owner_ok(store, passphrase, password)`: anyone at the admin screen while no spoken passphrase is set, otherwise the passphrase; on a sealed self the brain closes the spoken passphrase, and the owner's password (the one that seals the self, typed in its own field) opens it instead. On the emulator's sealed self: a change sent while Pebble ran was taken within 4 s with no restart, one sent while the brain was down was taken when it started, the held path saved in about 1 s with the brain paused by the owner and left it paused, and on the PC a sealed self with a spoken passphrase refused the passphrase and a wrong password and took the owner's password, both ways.

"Setup finished" now asks the tsunami question (Yes, No or Not now) and hands the answer to the brain's `finish_setup`. The brain reads its preferences once at start and would write its old copy back, so when an answer was saved and the brain is running, the keeper restarts the brain 45 s later (the brain notices the end of setup within 30 s), keeping the models. On a self already past setup the answer is not saved there; Around the house sets it.

**A new birth draw** (agency step 2; `brain/README.md`, Pebble's own settings, its birth and the gatekeeper). While the self is in setup and Pebble has no birthday yet, the Setup section shows the draw Pebble would be born with: its line ("I think I'm very playful ..."), the draw's number, each trait with the template's value beside any that differ, the free values and the first tastes. **Draw Pebble's temperament again** asks to confirm, holds the brain the same way, and calls the brain's own `pebble.android.seal.birth_draw(self_dir, True, passphrase)`, so the owner rule and the "only while it is being set up" rule are the brain's; the screen then shows the new draw, or the brain's reason. Outside setup, or on a sealed self (which the hook cannot open), nothing is offered. On the emulator, on a fresh self in setup: the button showed draw 1, the hold took about a second and draw 2 came back; on a self past setup the brain refused, and on the sealed self nothing was offered.

`setup_status`, `finish_setup` and `birth_draw` in `pebble/android/seal.py` take no bridge, so `SelfOps` no longer passes one to them: until now the Setup section always said "TypeError: setup_status() takes 1 positional argument but 2 were given". These seal helpers open the self without its key, so on a sealed self `setup_status` says setup is over whatever the self holds (the emulator's self, sealed on 15 October, still tells the brain it is being set up).

## The link to the body

- **Wi-Fi**: the brain connects to `tcp:<esp32>:7777` itself. Android's resolver does not answer `.local` names, so the app finds the ESP32 with Android's own service discovery (`_pebble._tcp`, which the firmware announces) and passes the address; or the owner types it on the admin screen.
- **USB cable** (bench): `UsbBridge` opens the ESP32's USB CDC port (usb-serial-for-android, 115200, DTR on) and offers it to the brain as `tcp:127.0.0.1:7778`, one client at a time, like the firmware's own TCP port.
- **The link key** (`link/BodyKey.kt`, `link/BodyPairing.kt`). The body obeys a phone only after it proves a shared 32-byte key (`interfaces.md`, "The link key, the handshake and tags"). The key is an HMAC-SHA256 key in the Android keystore (alias `pebble-body-link`, StrongBox first, the trusted environment if StrongBox refuses) and never leaves it. **Pair the body over the USB cable** has the keeper open the USB port (a bridge of its own if the link is Wi-Fi), take the port from the brain for a moment, make a random key, send it as `K <hex>`, keep it in the keystore, forget the raw bytes and run the handshake once to check the body proves it; then the brain restarts. The brain's link client (`pebble/link/auth.py`) reaches the key through Chaquopy (`BodyKey.has()`, `BodyKey.mac(bytes)`): one keystore call per connection works out that connection's session key, and every line after that is tagged in Python. Checked on the emulator: a key put in with the debug `BODYKEY` command (kept "in software" there; the Pixel has StrongBox), the brain proving it to a PC body on `10.0.2.2` and its tagged lines accepted, a PC body with another key refused every 5 s ("the body did not prove the shared key"), and a body listing `edge 2000` refused for driving.
- **Body profile** (`link/ProfileOverlay.kt`). `PUT /profile` keeps only the cameras' `fov_h_deg`, `rotate` and `mirror` and `drive.cpm` and `drive.wheel_track_mm`, within safe ranges, in the app's own storage (`files/body_overlay.json`); the brain service lays them over the app's `body_android.json` into `files/run/body.json` at each start. Nothing else from outside reaches the profile: no commands, no safety switches, no firmware settings. Checked on the emulator: a pushed file with `safety.enabled: false`, a shutdown command and `max_speed_mm_s` 5000 kept only the camera and `cpm` numbers.

## llama.cpp

`llama/build.sh` clones llama.cpp `v0.6.0` and builds `llama-server` with the NDK (28.2) for arm64-v8a and x86_64, as shared libraries with every CPU variant (`GGML_BACKEND_DL`, `GGML_CPU_ALL_VARIANTS`): at start it picks the best one for the phone's cores. The Pixel 6 Pro's Tensor chip is ARMv8.2: it has dot-product and half-precision maths but no int8 matrix multiply and no SVE, so it gets `android_armv8.2_2`, not the ARMv8.6 or ARMv9 variants. The executable ships as `libllama-server.so` so Android unpacks it into the app's native library folder, the only place an app may run a program from. The keeper starts it with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` set to that folder, on 127.0.0.1:8080 (main model, the four fastest cores: on the Pixel 6 Pro its two Cortex-X1 and two Cortex-A76) and 8081 (small model, the two slowest cores: two of its four Cortex-A55); it ranks the cores by their top clock speed, never by their numbers. The keeper restarts a server when it dies, kills it when it stops answering for 2 minutes or takes more than 15 minutes to load, and refuses to start a model that would not fit in memory (it says why on the admin screen).

**Settings the phone picks for itself** (`llm/LlamaPlan.kt`, `llm/Gguf.kt`, `llm/LlamaChoice.kt`). One slot per server (`-np 1`). The keeper reads the model file's header (layers, cache heads and their sizes, the sliding window, layers that share a cache) to work out the KV cache's bytes per token, then takes the highest rung whose memory fits: the model, the projector, 400 MB of working room, the KV cache and llama-server's RAM prompt cache (`--cache-ram`, which keeps each job's prompt so a turn, a reply or a drawing does not reread it). The main model leaves room for the small one at its lowest rung, under the same rules as before (both under 70 % of the phone's memory; each under free memory + 2 GB, otherwise it waits).

| Main rung | Window | Sliding layers | KV cache | RAM prompt cache |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 8,192 | whole (`--swa-full`) | f16 | 1,536 MiB |
| 1 | 8,192 | whole | f16 | 768 MiB |
| 2 | 8,192 | whole | q8_0 | 384 MiB |
| 3 | 6,144 | whole | f16 | 192 MiB |

The small model gets 2,048 tokens with 128 MiB, then 32 MiB. The main server also gets `--reasoning off`: llama-server turns Gemma 4's thinking on by default. For Gemma 4 E4B the cache is 448 MiB at 8,192 tokens with `--swa-full` (238 MiB at q8_0). Expected on the Pixel 6 Pro, not yet run there: rung 1 if Android reports at least about 11.2 GiB in all, otherwise rung 2; a Q8_0 projector (560 MB instead of 990 MB) makes rung 1 certain. If a server dies while loading, or within 5 minutes of being ready, or takes over 15 minutes to load, the next start uses the next lower rung; that is kept in `llama-main.json` / `llama-sub.json` in the no-backup folder, keyed by app version, phone model and model file. The admin status shows each server's window, rung, cache type and cache size. Why these settings: `reports/Making Pebble quicker to respond.md`.

**Batches and giving way.** Each server reads a prompt in batches (`-b` and `-ub`, the same size), 128 until the phone has measured its own (the talk bench below). A request that gives way, because the brain closes its connection when someone starts talking to Pebble, stops at the end of the batch being read, not at the end of the prompt, so the size of a batch is how long Pebble can be kept waiting. Checked on the emulator with the shipped x86_64 `llama-server` and a small stand-in model (LFM2.5-230M), from the closed connection to the next answer: 1.6 s at a batch of 512, about 0.5 s at 256, and 0.07 to 0.2 s at 64; a streamed request gave way no faster than a plain one. The cache count for the sliding layers follows the batch (`KvCost`, on any rung without `--swa-full`). The servers run with `--metrics`, which the keeper reads.

**The drafter.** When a drafter file sits next to the main model (Gemma 4's multi-token-prediction drafter: a GGUF with `mtp`, `assistant` or `draft` in its name, or the name typed on the admin screen), the main server starts with `--spec-type draft-mtp -md <file>`, and the memory check counts the file. It was never tried with the projector or `--swa-full`, so if the server stops while loading with it, or soon after, the next start goes without it and remembers why (`llama-main.json`, `"drafter": "failed"`); the memory rung is not touched. The talk bench keeps it only if the hot 20-token answer is at least 15 % faster with it, because reading a prompt may be slower with it (the pinned server asks the drafter for hidden states at every prompt position). Checked on the emulator with a stand-in that is not a drafter: llama-server said the model "doesn't contain MTP layers" and stopped, Pebble went on without it 5 s later, and the bench marked it failed. Not yet run with the real drafter, which was not fetched (about 100 MB).

**Checked model files** (`llm/ModelAllow.kt`, `llm/ModelImport.kt`). The language models live in the app's own storage (`files/models/`), where no other app, adb or USB file transfer can change them. The owner copies them into the drop folder in shared storage (`Android/data/nz.pebble.robot/files/models/`); the keeper hashes each settled file there with SHA-256, and copies it in only when its size and fingerprint are on `ModelAllow.KNOWN` (Gemma 4 E4B QAT, LFM2.5-230M, Gemma 4's drafter), then deletes the drop copy. An unknown file is never opened or parsed and stays where it is; the admin screen names it as refused, and the face says the model was refused when it is the main one. The receiver checks a model the same way as it arrives. Each internal file's fingerprint is remembered with its size and time (`files/models/.checked.json`), so it is hashed once, and a file that changes is checked again before any server starts on it. The projector auto-pick and the drafter auto-pick see only checked files. Checked on the emulator: the real LFM2.5 file moved in after checking, a junk `evil.gguf` and a cut-short Gemma file refused. The header reader (`llm/Gguf.kt`) refuses more than 1,024 layers, strings over 1 MB and absurd array lengths, and a broken header can no longer throw anything past `KvCost.read`.

**Model files** (`llm/ModelFiles.kt`). Before it starts a server the keeper checks the file: missing (it then says the exact name and folder, names a file of that name it finds one folder too high, a file whose name differs only in capitals, or the other GGUF files in the folder), or still being copied (changed within the last 15 s; `adb push` writes the file under its final name, so a server started then would load half a model). When llama-server stops while loading, the keeper reads that start's part of the model log: running out of memory, or being stopped by Android (code 137), steps the memory rung down as before; a damaged or cut-short file ("corrupted or incomplete"), an unknown kind of model or an unreadable projector does not, because a smaller setting would not help, and the problem says to copy the file again. A stop with the drafter still marks the drafter failed and is never blamed on the file. The talk bench starts only when the main model's files have settled and the model fits the memory, and a bench that measured nothing waits 30 minutes for that same file only, so a new file is measured at once. Each problem goes to `status.json` with a `kind` (`missing`, `copying`, `too_big`, `no_room_now`, `bad_file`, `stopped`, `no_program`), read by the face's line and the admin screen. Checked on the emulator: a missing file, one in `files/` instead of `files/models/`, a sparse 5.15 GB copy of the real header (too big for the emulator's 3 GB), a file just touched, and the first 60 MB of a real model (damaged: no step-down, retried with the usual waits).

**The admin screen's status** (`ui/AdminText.kt`) turns `status.json`, the face page's `/state`, `run/link.json` (where the brain service found the ESP32, or that it did not) and the brain log's last `link up` or `link down` line since the brain's last start into plain lines: the brain (a running brain never shows a stale reason), each model, the talk bench's step or results, memory search, the ears (listening, only its name, starting again and how often they stopped, Android's recognizer standing in), the eyes, the link to the body with what to check, heat and what Pebble does about it, storage (low under 2 GB) and each model file's size in bytes. Errors inside the admin screen's own Python calls go to `logs/admin.log` (the **Admin screen log** button), and the screen says only that something went wrong and nothing was changed. "Setup finished" and its passphrase field go once setup is over, and the second password field and **Seal Pebble's self** once the self is sealed.

**The talk bench** (`llm/TalkBench.kt`). At the first start, and again after an app update with a higher version code (`-Ppebble.versionCode`), a change of model, projector or drafter, or "Measure the eyes again", the keeper measures how fast the main model talks. It runs the model on its own port (8090) meanwhile, so the brain cannot get in its way; the brain waits as it does while a model loads. In order:
1. Reading speed at batches of 128, 64 and 512 (two fresh prompts of about 600 tokens each): the smallest within 5 % of the best is taken.
2. Giving way: a 1,500-token read is dropped after 1 s, and a one-token request is timed from the moment the connection closed. Over 2.5 s, the batch is halved (down to 32) and tried again.
3. Talking, with the drafter and without (when there is one): a 2,500-token prompt is read, then about 12 new tokens and the template's tail, and the 1st, 5th and 20th written tokens are timed.
4. Hot: 3 minutes of talk-like bursts (about 150 tokens read and 20 written, over and over), then the talking times again, with the phone's thermal headroom. The other drafter setting gets 1 more minute and is timed the same way. The drafter stays only if its hot 20-token time is at least 15 % better.
5. Memory: the resident memory of both servers (`VmRSS`) and the phone's free memory. Under 1.5 GB free with both running, the main model steps down one memory rung.

The results go to `llama-main.json` (`"bench"`), and the admin status shows them: first words (0.25 s for the turn to close, then the first 5 tokens, then 0.1 s for the voice), a short answer (20 tokens), the batch and the drafter. On the Pixel 6 Pro it should take 10 to 15 minutes, once; `"bench": false` in `pebble.json` switches it off and `"benchHotS"` sets the hot phase. On the emulator with the stand-in and a 20 s hot phase it took 80 to 86 s: a batch of 512 (199 tokens a second, against 173 at 128 and 153 at 64; giving way in 1.6 s), first words in 1.2 s and a 20-token answer in 1.7 s. The emulator has only 3 GB, so under 1.5 GB stayed free and it stepped the memory rung down, as it should.

**Heat and speed** (`keeper/Pace.kt`). Every 5 s the keeper reads Android's thermal status and headroom: warm at a headroom of 0.6 or the status "moderate", hot at 0.8 or "severe", and it steps back down only after 2 minutes cooler. Every minute it also reads the main server's counters and compares the last 10 minutes' writing speed with the bench's hot speed: 30 % slower is "slow". Both go to `run/pace.json`. The eyes read it: warm or slow takes their frame rate to 60 %, hot to 35 %, and the slow lane waits longer in step. The brain reads the same through `phoneHeat()` (headroom, status, level, slow) and can hold off its own background work. The models are not restarted for heat: a restart throws away the warm prompt, and the next answer would reread all of it, which costs far more than the heat saves; the bench's hot numbers already set the drafter and the batch. Checked on the emulator by forcing the thermal status (`cmd thermalservice override-status 3`): the level went to hot at the next keeper tick and the eyes' pace to 0.35.

On the emulator both servers (LFM2.5-230M Q8_0, 247 MB) were ready 6 s after starting and the brain's first turn ran on them (652-token prompt at 50 tokens/s, answers at 5 to 9 tokens/s on an SSE4.2-only emulator).

**Pictures.** When a projector file (`mmproj*.gguf`) is next to the main model, the main server starts with `--mmproj` and llama.cpp's multimodal support (`mtmd`), and takes pictures in the OpenAI style (`image_url` with a `data:image/jpeg;base64,...` URL). The brain gets the picture from `eyesSnapshot` (a JPEG of the newest frame, at most 768 px on its long side, made in about 10 ms). `/props` reports `modalities.vision`. The admin screen's projector field says `auto` (find it), `none`, or a file name, and the memory check counts the projector too. On the emulator, SmolVLM-256M with its projector answered about a picture in 58 s the first time and 24 s after that; the image encoder is most of it, and it is far faster with AVX or on a phone. Not measured yet with Gemma 4 E4B on the Pixel 6 Pro.

**GPU.** CPU only. The Pixel 6 Pro's GPU is an Arm Mali-G78 (20 cores), not a Qualcomm Adreno. llama.cpp's OpenCL backend is written and tested for Adreno only (its own page lists Adreno chips and does not mention Mali), so it is no use here. Its Vulkan backend can run on Mali, but nobody has shown it faster than the CPU on this phone, and a GPU backend would also share the phone's memory bandwidth with the eyes and add heat. Not worth building in; at most one evening's experiment with `-DGGML_VULKAN=ON`.

`am instrument` stops the app first, so the last line brings the face back. Don't use `gradlew connectedDebugAndroidTest`: it uninstalls the app afterwards, and the emulator's self with it.

The brain's Python is packaged from `../brain/pebble` at build time (`syncBrain`); the eye, sound and speech models from `../brain/pebble/eyes/models`, `../brain/pebble/hearing/models` and `../brain/pebble/speech/models` go into the APK's assets (`syncModels`; `-Ppebble.speechModels=<folder>` uses another speech folder). The release build is signed with the owner's key from `~/.pebble/signing/keystore.properties`, or from the file `PEBBLE_SIGNING_PROPERTIES` names (SETUP.md says how to make one); without it the build warns and signs with the debug key. `ADMIN` opens the admin screen without the PIN. `BIRTH` shows the birth draw (`op status`) or asks for another with the brain held (`op again`, with `--es passphrase` when one is set; `--es self <folder>` as for `HOUSE`). `HOUSE` reads (`op status`), saves with the brain held (`op save --es json '{"town": "Wellington"}'`, with `--es passphrase` when one is set) or finishes setup (`op finish --es tsunami yes`); `--es self <folder>` works on another self under the app's files, which a fresh one starts in setup. `VECTORS` reads the sound model's numbers through the brain's own reader (`--ei idle_s 65` then checks they are dropped). `CLOSEUP` takes a close look (`--ez save true` keeps it as `files/probe/closeup.jpg`, for the emulator only); with the simulated eyes it starts the phone's eyes for the probe, as `FLOOR` does. `TORCH` switches the light on for a moment, with the named camera open first. `HIGHBAND` lists the high band's rows and the microphone plan (`--ez bench true` times bringing 48 kHz down and the high band instead). `MICPROBE` runs the microphone probe (it plays clicks and a sweep; options `seconds`, `rates`, `layouts`, `sources`, `clicks`, `sweep`, `click_level`, `sweep_level`). `SCORES` keeps the sound scores with their place in the test loop in `files/probe/scores_<tag>.json`, to compare two test sounds. A test sound may be 16, 32, 48 or 96 kHz, mono or stereo; `tools/hearing_fixtures.py --wide <in> <out> --junk` makes a 48 kHz copy of a 16 kHz recording with tones and hiss above 9 kHz added. Every debug command lets go of its broadcast at once and carries on in the background.

`EARS` shows the ears' state, and with `--ez end true` ends the ears process the way a native `exit()` would, to show the brain carries on; `--ei during_voice 2 --ei after_ms 300` waits for the second `voice_start` and ends them 300 ms into it, to check a cut sentence is heard again. `MAP` opens the map screen without the PIN. `MEASURE` does what "Measure the eyes again" does.

`RECALL` checks the memory search's sentence model against the PC: it reads `recall_cases.json` from the app's external files folder (`adb push app/src/test/resources/recall/cases.json /sdcard/Android/data/nz.pebble.robot/files/recall_cases.json`), compares the phone's word-piece ids and numbers with the PC's (the lowest cosine per group), times a memory, a question and a batch of 32 (`--ei repeat`), and runs one batch through the brain's own `PhoneSentences`. `--ez bench true` measures it now as at the first start, or waits for the bench already running (`--ez eyes true` starts the phone's eyes for it when the brain runs other eyes, as `FLOOR` does; `--ez settle false` skips waiting for the eyes and ears). `--ez gate true` asks one question and numbers one batch of 8 while you make a language model write or force the phone hot (`adb shell cmd thermalservice override-status 3`), and says which waited, which gave up and when. `--ez preempt true` starts a batch of 32 long memories and asks a question `--ei after_ms` into it (`--ez ask false` asks none, for starting a language model's writing by hand instead), and says how long each took and how often the batch gave way.

`HEAR` logs every speech event with its latency against a test recording (config `"testAudio": "<file>.wav"`, a 16 kHz mono WAV in the app's external files folder, looped every 15 s), and can answer each final to time the first spoken word; `--es config '<json>'` passes speech settings and `--es name_end_s 1.57` times the name spotter. `BARGE` times a stop in the middle of a sentence. `ECHO` makes Pebble speak five sentences and counts what the VAD and the recognizer heard of its own voice (the echo check for a real phone). `SNAP` times a snapshot. `MOUTHS` reads the lips in pictures copied to `files/mouths/<kind>/*.jpg`. `FLOOR` asks for one floor scan, then says Pebble is moving for the given seconds, and logs the scans, their times, how many sights carried them, the motion and a place print; when the brain runs the simulated eyes (`--sim`) it starts the phone's eyes from the android profile for the probe and closes them after (`--ez keep true` leaves them on). `DEPTH` runs the depth model and the rays on pictures copied to `files/floor/` (a roll in the name, `_r6.png`, levels by 6 degrees) and writes each depth map and its rays next to the picture, to compare with the PC. `MOTION` measures camera motion between shifted crops of a sample picture.

On the emulator, run the simulated body on the PC (`python -m pebble.sim --host 127.0.0.1 --port 7777`) and set the ESP32 address to `10.0.2.2`; the phone's eyes then run on the emulator's cameras, or on a test picture (`"testImage": "portrait.jpg"`).

### Not verified until a real phone

Language models are not in the APK: Gemma 4 (Gemma terms of use) and LFM2.5 (Liquid AI's licence) are copied onto the phone by the owner.

Python is 3.13 because Chaquopy 17 needs the PC's Python to match and has cryptography 42 and numpy for it. Chaquopy has no onnxruntime at all and OpenCV only for Python 3.10 (4.5.1, too old for YuNet 2023), so the models run in Kotlin rather than in Python.
