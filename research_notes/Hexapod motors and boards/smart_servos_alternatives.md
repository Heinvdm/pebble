# Smart servos and small geared actuators as an alternative to brushless FOC joints (12-joint mini hexapod)

Research date: 2026-10-17. Labels used below: **[official]** = maker's own data (ROBOTIS e-manual source files, Feetech's own GitHub library); **[seller]** = retailer or reseller listing; **[community]** = forum post, build log or third-party wiki; **[estimate]** = my own calculation from the cited numbers. Unit conversions: 1 kg·cm = 0.0981 N·m; rpm × 0.1047 = rad/s.

How the sources were reached: the sandbox could not open emanual.robotis.com, feetechrc.com, waveshare.com, robotshop.com or most retail sites directly. ROBOTIS figures come from the e-manual's own source files on GitHub (the same data the e-manual pages are built from). Feetech registers and licences come from Feetech's official GitHub library. Retail prices and most Feetech, Hiwonder, Lynxmotion and Kondo specs come from search-result extracts of seller pages, which I could not open to check in full. The web-search budget ran out before I could search for noise data, the STS3215 speed figures, NZD prices and new 2025–2026 ROBOTIS models. Those are listed as gaps.

Requirement used throughout (from the brief): swing joint 0.10–0.15 N·m; lift joint 0.13 N·m steady and 0.35 N·m peak; 6 rad/s (57 rpm) running and 10 rad/s (95.5 rpm) during a hop push; 25–30 g per joint. Mechanical power at the hop point is 0.35 N·m × 10 rad/s = **3.5 W per joint** [estimate].

## 1. ROBOTIS Dynamixel: XL330, XC330, XM335, XL430 and 2XL430 specs, modes, protocol, bus rate, price and licence

### Takeaway
The X330 family (XL330 18 g, XC330 23 g, 20 × 34 × 26 mm) is the only Dynamixel line that fits the 25–30 g joint budget. It has true current control and current-based position modes, a 12-bit absolute encoder, a bus watchdog and an Apache-2.0 SDK. No model reaches 0.35 N·m at 10 rad/s. The best 5–6 V model under load (XC330-M181) does about 5.6 rad/s at 0.35 N·m on 5 V and about 7.6 rad/s on 6 V. The X330 current reading is measured at the supply input, not in the motor phases, so it is only a rough torque signal. There is no XC330-M077. The newer XM335-T323 (27 g) has a full metal case but is too slow.

### Inferences
- [estimate] Linear speed–torque model, ω(T) = ω0·(1 − T/Tstall), from the official stall and no-load points. Speed at 0.35 N·m: XL330-M288 3.5 rad/s @5 V and 5.4 @6 V. XC330-M181 **5.6 @5 V, 7.6 @6 V**. XC330-M288 5.3 @5 V, 6.9 @6 V. XC330-T181 4.0 @9 V, 6.7 @12 V. XC330-T288 2.9 @9 V, 4.8 @12 V. XM335 2.7 @9 V, 4.1 @12 V. XL430 3.2 @9 V. Torque available at 10 rad/s: XL330-M077 0.16 N·m @5 V; XC330-M181 0.16 @5 V and 0.25 @6 V; XC330-T181 0.12 @12 V. All others are ≤ 0.13 or cannot reach 10 rad/s at all.
- [estimate] Peak mechanical output (Tstall·ω0/4): XL330-M288 1.4 W @5 V. XC330-M181 2.0 W @5 V and 2.7 W @6 V. XC330-M288 2.8 W @6 V. XC330-T181 2.4 W @12 V. All are below the **3.5 W** the 0.35 N·m @ 10 rad/s point needs, so no Dynamixel in the weight class can meet that point as written.
- [estimate] Running at 0.13 N·m: XC330-M181 gives 10.6 rad/s @5 V, XL330-M288 8.1 rad/s @5 V, and XC330-T181 7.0 rad/s @9 V. All meet the 6 rad/s running target.
- Because X330 current is sensed at the supply input, input current ≈ duty × phase current. When holding a pose at low speed, the reading under-reports torque, so current-based position mode on these servos limits input current rather than motor torque. A "spring" made this way will feel stiffer when moving fast and softer when nearly still. It needs calibrating with a load cell or a known weight.
- Stop handling maps well onto the X330 registers: a broadcast write of Torque Enable = 0 (limp), or of Goal Current = small (soft hold). The Bus Watchdog stops the servos by itself if the ESP32 stops sending.
- [estimate] Bus timing at 4 Mbps: one Fast Sync Read of position + velocity + current (10 bytes × 12 servos, about 180-byte reply) plus one Sync Write of goal positions (about 75 bytes) is roughly 300 bytes ≈ 0.75 ms of wire time, with Return Delay Time set to 0. That makes 500 Hz–1 kHz plausible on a native ESP32-S3 UART, which avoids the USB latency problem. This has not been measured.

## 2. Feetech STS3032, STS3215, STS3046, SCS0009, SCS2332, SM/HL series, Waveshare ST3215/ST3020: specs, load/current feedback, torque-limited mode and libraries

### Takeaway
Feetech's small STS3032 and SCS2332 (about 20 g, 23.2 × 12.1 × 28.5 mm, 4.5 kg·cm ≈ 0.44 N·m stall @6 V, about 111 rpm) are the only Feetech parts in the weight class. They report position, speed, PWM "load", voltage, temperature and present current. They offer only a torque (PWM) limit, not a real current or torque mode. The well-documented STS3215, ST3215 and ST3020 servos are 55 g-class "standard" servos and twice the per-joint budget. Feetech's HL series adds a true constant-current mode but is not documented at small size. The official Feetech libraries (FTServo for Arduino/ESP32, Python and Linux) are MIT. The Waveshare-derived SCServo code is GPL-3.0.

### Inferences
- [estimate] STS3032 / SCS2332 at 6 V, linear model: 8.2 rad/s at 0.13 N·m (meets running), 2.4 rad/s at 0.35 N·m, 0.06 N·m at 10 rad/s. Peak mechanical power is about 1.3 W, about the same as an XL330-M288 at 5 V. The 0.13 N·m steady lift load is about 88 % of the STS3032's 0.15 N·m rated torque, so they would run warm on lift joints. They are acceptable on swing joints.
- SCS0009 (0.074 N·m rated) is too weak for either joint. It could suit only very light non-load joints such as a head tilt.
- The Feetech "torque limit" caps output effort (PWM) rather than regulating current. It protects gears in a stall, but it is a poorer "spring" than Dynamixel current-based position mode, and present current would have to be read and used in an outer loop on the ESP32. Only the HL series has a built-in current loop, and no small HL model was found.
- The SCS2332 uses a potentiometer (300°, 10-bit), which wears and gives less absolute range than the magnetic STS3032. Prefer the STS3032 if Feetech is chosen.
- [estimate] At 1 Mbps (Feetech max), a sync read of 12 servos × about 15 bytes plus sync write is roughly 3 ms of wire time, before per-servo reply delays. That suggests about 200–300 Hz control. It is unmeasured.

- No price was found for STS3032 or SCS0009.
- The Feetech "SM" series (RS-485 / brushless) could not be researched (no search budget). From the library, only SMS_STS/SCSCL/HLSCL classes were seen.
- Whether STS3032 firmware supports mode 2/3 (PWM or step) and a protection-current register like the larger STS models is unconfirmed.

## 3. Other options: Hiwonder, Lynxmotion LSS, Kondo, Mini Pupper custom servos, N20/N30 gearmotors with encoders

### Takeaway
None of the other bus servos fit the weight class. Hiwonder LX-224/HTD-35H (57–63 g), Lynxmotion LSS (about 80 g) and Kondo KRS-2572HV (47.7 g) are 2–3× too heavy and mostly lack current feedback. An N20 gearmotor with a magnetic encoder is light, but it needs a custom driver, current sensing and an output-side absolute sensor, which makes it a DIY smart servo. Small quadrupeds such as Mini Pupper use custom low-cost servos with position-only feedback.

## 4. Which meet 0.35 N·m peak and 10 rad/s at 5 V or 9 V; power rail and regulator; how they fit six hips (footprint sketch)

### Takeaway
None meets 0.35 N·m and 10 rad/s together. The requirement point needs 3.5 W per joint, and the best weight-class servo (XC330-M181 @6 V) peaks at about 2.7 W. If the hop push can work at about 0.35 N·m and 6–7 rad/s, or about 0.25 N·m at 10 rad/s, then **XC330-M181 at 6 V** on lift joints and **XL330-M288 or XC330-M181** on swing joints is the closest fit, at about 246–276 g for 12. The 5–6 V servos need a buck converter from the 9 V bank (about 6 V, 8–10 A peak). XC330-T181 can run straight from 9 V but is slower (4 rad/s at 0.35 N·m). The 20 × 34 × 26 mm X330 body fits the 52 mm hip pitch only if the lift servo sits close to the swing axis. Check this in `cad/check.py`.

- What passes: running (0.13 N·m at 6 rad/s) is met by XL330-M288, XC330-M181, XC330-M288, XC330-T181 and STS3032. Swing (0.15 N·m at 6 rad/s) is met by the same, plus XL330-M077. **Nobody passes the hop point.** The closest are XC330-M181 @6 V (0.35 N·m at about 7.6 rad/s, or 10 rad/s at 0.25 N·m) and XC330-M288 @6 V (0.35 N·m at 6.9 rad/s with more stall margin for landings, 1.10 N·m stall).
- Landing loads are mostly absorbed (negative work, near-zero speed), so the 0.35 N·m peak at landing is well within XC330 stall (0.60–1.10 N·m). The hard part is the push, which needs torque and speed at once. Either lower the hop target, use a lighter body, or use a parallel spring on the lift joint to carry the 0.13 N·m static load.
- **Power rail**: the bank's 9 V is above the 6.0 V maximum for XL330/XC330-M/STS3032, so a buck converter is needed. Supercaps could sit on the 9 V side of the buck, where stored energy ½CV² is about 2.25× higher per farad than at 6 V. A 9 V→6 V buck rated about 5 A continuous and 10 A peak (60 W) would cover the stated 40–60 W, 0.1 s bursts. Twelve XC330-M at 6 V all stalled would draw 12 × 2.15 A = 25.8 A, which is far above this, so set per-servo Current Limit (38) values so that the sum stays within the buck rating. For example, about 0.8 A each gives 9.6 A total.
- [estimate] Static holding power is small. For XC330-M181 at 5 V, phase resistance including the driver is about 5 V / 1.80 A ≈ 2.8 Ω, and kt ≈ 0.33 N·m/A at the output. Holding 0.13 N·m needs about 0.39 A, so I²R ≈ 0.42 W per lift joint, or about 2.5 W for six. That is comfortably within the 5 V 3 A or 9 V 2.5 A bank.
- **Direct 9 V option**: XC330-T181 sits inside its 6.5–12 V range at 9 V, so it needs no servo regulator and supercaps can sit directly on the 9 V bus. The cost is speed: 7.0 rad/s at 0.13 N·m and 4.0 at 0.35 N·m. A 9→12 V boost would raise that to 9.9 / 6.7 rad/s. XC330-T also uses 5 V logic, so the ESP32-S3's 3.3 V UART needs a level-shifting buffer.

**Footprint sketch** (top view, mm; X330 = 20 W × 34 H × 26 D, output axis along D) [estimate]:
```
 x = -52            x = 0             x = +52
 +------+          +------+          +------+   y = +44..48 (hip axis)
 |swing |  32 mm   |swing |  32 mm   |swing |   swing servo, axis vertical:
 |20x34 |<-------->|20x34 |<-------->|20x34 |   20 (x) x 34 (y) on deck, 26 tall
 +------+          +------+          +------+
 |         body deck 140 x 80 (y = -40..+40)         |

```
- With swing servos oriented with their 34 mm side along y, the gap between neighbours is 52 − 20 = 32 mm. Turned the other way, it is 52 − 34 = 18 mm.
- The lift servo rides on the swing horn and sweeps ±25°. Its axis is horizontal and along x, so it is about 26 mm wide in x, or about 34 mm with horn and idler bracket. The inner corner moves toward the neighbouring hip by (c + 10)·sin 25° + 17·cos 25°, where c is the distance from the swing axis to the lift servo's centre. For c = 0 this is 19.6 mm per leg, leaving a 13 mm gap when two neighbours swing toward each other. For c = 10 it is 23.9 mm (4 mm gap). For c ≥ 15 the bodies touch at 52 mm spacing. So the lift servo should sit almost over the swing axis (stacked), giving a stack height of about 26 + 3 + 20 ≈ 50 mm above or below the deck, or the front and rear legs should splay outward. Verify with `cad/check.py`.
- The hips at y = ±44–48 sit 4–8 mm outside the 80 mm deck edge, so the swing servos (34 mm long) would sit about half inboard on the deck.

### Gaps
- Real speed–torque curves are not exactly linear (ROBOTIS publishes performance graphs that were not captured), so the estimates above may be ±15 %.
- No measured numbers exist for X330 or STS3032 thermal limits under repeated hops (continuous-torque ratings are not published for X330).

## 5. Real-world reports: small legged robots on XL330, XC330, STS3215 or STS3032; gear wear and stripping, noise, backlash, current-based "spring" behaviour

### Takeaway
The best evidence is Open Duck Mini, an RL-walking biped about 42 cm tall. v1 started on XL330-M288 legs, moved to XC330-M288 as "way more powerful", and had "too much play at some joints". v2 moved to STS3215. LeRobot's SO-100/101 arms make STS3215 the most-used hobby smart servo, with anecdotal gear stripping and burned servos after stalls. No XL330/XC330 hexapod build log was found. No noise data was found. Compliance through current-based position mode is used in practice, but needs tuning per joint.

### Inferences
- The Open Duck experience suggests XL330-M288 is under-powered for legs carrying a body and hopping, and that 330-size servos have noticeable play. Expect it at the foot as several mm of slop through a 45 + 60 mm leg. Use XC330 (metal gears, 2 bearings) for lift joints at least.
- 181:1 to 288:1 spur gearboxes are not easily back-driven, so landing shocks go into the gear teeth rather than being absorbed by the motor. Gimbal motors with FOC (direct or low ratio) avoid this. With servos, use a soft current limit during landings, a compliant foot or leg spring, and a shock-absorbing horn mount, and keep hops small.
- Noise: coreless motors with metal spur gears tend to whine at speed. Plastic gears (XL330) are usually quieter but weaker. This is general knowledge and not measured for these models, so a test is needed before committing, given the desk-pet requirement.

## 6. Total cost and mass for 12 (+2 spares), and the adapters needed (U2D2 or an ESP32-S3 half-duplex interface)

### Takeaway
The best-fitting set (6 × XL330-M288 swing + 6 × XC330-M181 lift + spares) weighs about 246 g for the 12 joints. It costs about €280 for the XL330s at EU retail plus about $724 for the XC330s at US retail, so roughly $1,000+ before brackets, a buck converter and an interface. An all-XC330 set is about $1,450 and 276 g. An all-XL330 set is about €560 and 216 g, but is too weak for hops. A Feetech STS3032 set would weigh about 240 g, but its price was not found and it has no real current mode. Only a $32 U2D2 (for PC setup) and a 3-state buffer on the ESP32-S3 UART are needed.
