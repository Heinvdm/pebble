## 1. ESP32-S3 boards and modules: size, mounting, USB, GPIO, memory, regulator, price, openness

### Takeaway
None of the small ESP32-S3 boards (XIAO, Zero, SuperMini, QT Py, TinyS3) has mounting holes. They are castellated or header boards that need a carrier PCB or a printed cradle. For a 140 × 80 mm deck, the best fits are the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero (23.5 × 18 mm, castellated, 800 mA LDO, about 24 GPIOs) and the Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Plus (21 × 17.8 mm, 16 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM, about 20 GPIOs counting its extra castellations). For a publishable custom board, the ESP32-S3-MINI-1 (15.4 × 20.5 mm) or the WROOM-1 (18 × 25.5 mm) module soldered straight onto the body PCB is the clean end state. The Waveshare ESP32-S3-Tiny has no USB port, so it cannot plug into the phone without an adapter. The DevKitC-1 is too big.

### Inferences
- **GPIO budget [E]:** the body board needs about 20 signals: USB D+/D− (GPIO19/20), one SPI bus for the IMU and CAN-FD controller(s) (SCK, MOSI, MISO plus 2–3 CS and 2–3 INT, so 7–9), TWAI TX/RX plus transceiver standby (3), I²C for the ToF and power monitor (2), ToF XSHUT (1), power-monitor ALERT (1), heartbeat out to the safety MCU and status back in (2), and an LED (1). The base XIAO's 11 header GPIOs are too few. The XIAO Plus (~20) is just enough. The Zero (~24) has some margin. The S3 chip (45) on a module-down PCB has plenty.
- **USB to the phone [E]:** the boards with a single USB-C wired to native USB (Zero, XIAO, SuperMini, QT Py, TinyS3; prior knowledge, consistent with the Zero docs' "hold BOOT while plugging in USB") give a direct phone-to-ESP32 CDC link with no bridge chip. Avoid boards whose USB-C goes to a UART bridge, or check which port is which (the DevKitC-1 has both).
- **Mounting [E]:** since none of the compact boards has holes, design a small carrier "body board" (for example 60 × 40 mm with 4 × M2 holes) that takes a castellated Zero or XIAO Plus, the IMU, CAN, power monitor and safety parts. That is easier for other builders to make than a module-down design. A WROOM-1 can be hand-soldered (edge castellations); the MINI-1 and PICO-1 have pads underneath that really need reflow.
- **Power headroom [E]:** with an IMU, ToF, CAN-FD controller, transceivers and the radio, 3V3 draw can pass 300 mA in Wi-Fi bursts. The Zero's 800 mA LDO and the TinyS3's 700 mA LDO are comfortable. The XIAO's LDO rating is unknown.
- Mass is negligible for every candidate (1–5 g), so it should not decide the choice.

## 2. ESP32-S3 TWAI (CAN), CAN-FD options, transceivers, and USB CDC alongside a fast motor bus

### Takeaway
The ESP32-S3 has one TWAI controller. It is classic CAN 2.0 only, up to 1 Mbit/s, and it **treats CAN-FD frames as errors**, so it cannot even sit on a bus carrying FD traffic. One classic CAN bus at 1 Mbit/s cannot exchange a command and a reply with 12 joints at 500–1000 Hz (about 300 Hz is the ceiling). For 500–1000 Hz you need CAN-FD through an SPI controller (MCP2518FD, 1 Mbit/s arbitration and up to 8 Mbit/s data), ideally two buses with 6 joints each, or joints that accept one broadcast command frame. Native USB CDC to the phone uses separate hardware and can run at the same time, but it is a bulk, 1 ms-frame link, so the fast loop must close on the ESP32, not on the phone.

### Cited Findings
- The ESP32-S3 has 1 TWAI controller; "The TWAI controllers on the ESP32-S3 are not compatible with FD format frames and will interpret such frames as errors." The same API page lists FD fields (e.g. `brs`) shared with other chips, which don't apply to the S3. [O] [ESP-IDF TWAI (v5.5)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/twai.html)
- TWAI is ISO 11898-1 / CAN 2.0 compatible, standard and extended IDs, 25 kbit/s to 1 Mbit/s. [S] [Ineltek TWAI overview](https://docs.ineltek.com/docs/two-wire-automotive-interface-twai-can/); [S] [Zephyr WeAct docs](https://docs.zephyrproject.org/latest/boards/weact/esp32s3_b/doc/index.html)
- Typical rates 125/250/500 kbit/s and 1 Mbit/s; a 3.3 V transceiver is needed for a real bus. [S] [espp/twai component readme](https://components.espressif.com/components/espp/twai/versions/1.2.1/readme)
- A vendor board pairs the S3's classic TWAI with an MCP2518FD for CAN-FD. [S] [Copperhill ESP32-S3 board](https://copperhilltech.com/esp32s3-5-480x854-ips-lcd-with-can-fd-and-classical-can-ports/)
- **MCP2518FD:** arbitration up to 1 Mbit/s, data phase up to 8 Mbit/s, SPI up to 20 MHz [S] [Mouser MCP2518FD](https://www.mouser.com/new/microchip/microchip-mcp2518fd-controllers); Microchip's comparison page lists a 17 MHz SPI limit for one variant [O] [Microchip MCP2517FD/MCP2518FD comparison](https://microchip.com/en-us/product-comparison.mcp2517fd.mcp2518fd); 14-pin SOIC, "8 Mbps" [S] [RS Online](https://ie.rs-online.com/web/p/can-interface-ics/2326035).
- MCP2518FD breakout (The Pi Hut "CAN Transceiver MCP2518 Board"): integrated ATA6563 transceiver, SPI, works with ESP32, 120 Ω termination on a solder pad. [S] [The Pi Hut](https://thepihut.com/products/can-transceiver-mcp2518-board)
- Soldered's ESP-IDF MCP2518FD component handles CAN 2.0 and FD. It needs a retry loop at power-up, because the chip needs a moment before it answers on SPI. It defaults to classic mode, so normal (FD) mode must be set before `begin`. The board uses a 20 MHz oscillator. [S] [Espressif component registry, Soldered MCP2518FD](https://components.espressif.com/components/solderedelectronics/soldered-mcp2518fd-esp-idf-component/versions/1.0.0/readme)
- LilyGO T-2CAN-FD example: ESP32-S3 + MCP2518FD at 500 kbit/s arbitration and 2 Mbit/s data. [O] [LilyGO wiki](https://wiki.lilygo.cc/products/industrial-series/t-2can-fd/quick-start.html)

[O] [Adafruit Learn: CAN Pal](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-can-pal); [S] [The Pi Hut Adafruit list](https://thepihut.com/collections/adafruit-breakout-boards/brand_adafruit)

[S] [Reach / moteus r4.11](https://m.reach.dog/shop/mjbots-robotic-systems/products/moteus-r4-11); [S] [hackaday.io mjbots quad](https://hackaday.io/project/167845-mjbots-quad); [S] [mjbots quad logs](https://hackaday.io/project/167845/logs)
- **USB:** ESP32-S2/S3 OTG is full speed (12 Mbit/s); the bulk theory is 64 bytes per 1 ms frame per endpoint (about 512 kB/s), and "bulk transfers do not reduce latency". [O] [Espressif USB-OTG overview](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-iot-solution/en/latest/usb/usb_overview/usb_otg.html). A forum user measured about 4.25 Mbit/s CDC TX on an S3; an Espressif staffer put the practical maximum at about 9.1 Mbit/s. [S] [esp32.com thread](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=100435)
- The S3 has two USB device paths on the same pins: the ROM USB-Serial/JTAG console, which is "incompatible with TinyUSB", or TinyUSB on USB-OTG with its own CDC. Reconfiguring the USB pins or sleeping makes the CDC device vanish. [O] [ESP-IDF USB OTG console](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5/esp32s3/api-guides/usb-otg-console.html); [O] [esp-faq USB](https://github.com/espressif/esp-faq/blob/a71fa4f/docs/en/software-framework/peripherals/usb.rst)
- In Arduino-ESP32, a 2023 bug (from IDF 4.4.3) meant that in USB-OTG (TinyUSB) mode, uploads needed a manual BOOT+reset; it is not confirmed fixed. [S] [arduino-esp32 PR #7828](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/pull/7828); PlatformIO USB-mode settings: [S] [ThingPulse](https://thingpulse.com/usb-settings-for-logging-with-the-esp32-s3-in-platformio/)
- The S3 has 3 UARTs, each configurable; the maximum is the driver macro `UART_BITRATE_MAX` (value not in the extract). [O] [ESP-IDF UART (S3)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.2.3/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/uart.html). On the original ESP32, SPI through the GPIO matrix is limited to about 40 MHz, and 80 MHz needs the IO-MUX pins. [O] [ESP-IDF v3 SPI master](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v3.1.7/api-reference/peripherals/spi_master.html)

### Inferences
- **Bus time budget [E]:** a classic CAN 2.0A frame with 8 data bytes is 111 bits plus up to about 24 stuff bits, so about 111–135 µs at 1 Mbit/s. A command and a reply for each of 12 joints is 24 frames, about 2.7–3.2 ms per cycle, so the ceiling is roughly 310–370 Hz at 100 % bus load, and about 250 Hz at a sane 70–80 %. Even packing 4 joints' setpoints into one frame (3 commands + 12 replies) still needs about 1.7–2.0 ms, so 500 Hz is out of reach on one classic bus. This matches the moteus RS-485 figure of 200 Hz for 12 servos on one bus.
- **CAN-FD budget [E]:** at 1 Mbit/s arbitration and 5 Mbit/s data, a 64-byte broadcast frame takes about 160–170 µs and a 16-byte reply about 70–80 µs. One broadcast plus 12 replies is about 1.1 ms (about 900 Hz at 100 %). Two FD buses of 6 joints each take about 0.6 ms per cycle, so **1 kHz at about 60 % load**. With per-joint command frames instead of a broadcast, the budget roughly doubles. This depends on the joint boards' protocol (another researcher covers those).
- **Practical design [E]:** run the gait engine at 1 kHz on the ESP32. Send joint setpoints at 500 Hz to 1 kHz only if the joints are CAN-FD on two MCP2518FD buses. With classic CAN joints, send at about 250 Hz and let each joint's own position loop (tens of kHz on FOC boards) interpolate. The TWAI then stays free for classic accessories, or as a second classic bus.
- **SPI load [E]:** at 20 MHz SPI, moving a 16–24-byte CAN-FD message to or from the MCP2518FD takes about 10 µs of wire time, so 24 messages per cycle is under 0.5 ms per millisecond. Feasible, but use DMA and batched transactions, and put the IMU on its own SPI host or chip select so IMU reads are not stuck behind CAN traffic.
- **USB and CAN together [E]:** USB-OTG, TWAI and SPI are separate peripherals, so running them at the same time is a scheduling question, not a hardware conflict. Pin the gait, IMU and bus task to one core at high priority, leave TinyUSB/USB-Serial-JTAG and Wi-Fi (if used) on the other, and keep USB traffic to about 50–200 Hz of state and commands (a few hundred bytes each, far below the measured 4 Mbit/s). Prefer the hardware USB-Serial/JTAG CDC for simplicity and reliable flashing, unless you need custom USB descriptors.
- **Transceiver choice [E]:** for FD at 5 Mbit/s with only 3.3 V logic, use a TJA1051T/3-class part with VIO = 3.3 V and a small 5 V rail (as the CAN Pal does). The SN65HVD230 boards are classic-CAN parts and fine only for the TWAI at 1 Mbit/s.

### Gaps
- No TCAN1462 or MCP2562FD data came back from search. From prior knowledge, unverified: both are 5 V-VCC, VIO-pin FD transceivers rated well beyond 2 Mbit/s; TI's TCAN3413/3414 are 3.3 V-only FD parts. Check the datasheets.
- The official TWAI maximum (1 Mbit/s) comes from a third-party summary; the ESP32-S3 TRM was not reachable.
- No measured ESP32-S3 + MCP2518FD SPI latency was found; benchmark it.
- No measured USB CDC round-trip latency on an S3 with an Android host was found.
- No dimensions found for any MCP2518FD breakout or for the Adafruit CAN Pal.

## 3. IMUs: ranges, noise, ODR, interface, fusion, breakouts, price

### Takeaway
For balance at 1 kHz or more and hard landings, choose a 6-axis SPI IMU with at least ±16 g, preferably ±24–32 g. The **TDK ICM-45686** (±32 g, ±4000 dps, 3.8 mdps/√Hz, 0.42 mA) is the strongest new-design part, but it has almost no ready breakouts. The **ICM-42688-P** (2.8 mdps/√Hz, 70 µg/√Hz, SPI 24 MHz, gyro ODR to 32 kHz) is the most proven but stops at ±16 g. For prototyping, the **Adafruit LSM6DSO32** (±32 g, SPI, US$12.50) or **SparkFun Micro LSM6DSV16X** (19.05 × 7.62 mm, US$25.50) are buyable now. The **BMI088** (±24 g, Bosch's drone and robot IMU, strong vibration rejection) is the robust alternative. The BNO085 is unsuitable for the fast loop: fused output tops out at 400 Hz, and it reads ±8 g per a third-party driver.

### Inferences
- **Range for landings [E]:** a small hexapod dropping a few centimetres onto stiff printed feet sees short impact spikes that can exceed 16 g at the body. Clipping corrupts the impact detection and the attitude update at exactly the wrong moment. Measure first with a ±32 g part (the LSM6DSO32 breakout). If peaks stay under about 12 g, the ICM-42688-P is fine; otherwise use the ICM-45686 (±32 g) or the BMI088 (±24 g).
- **Rate [E]:** every 6-axis candidate except the BNO085 delivers 1 kHz or more of gyro data. Use the FIFO with a data-ready interrupt and read in bursts over SPI. The phone's own IMU cannot serve the balance loop, because the USB bulk link adds ≥1 ms of jitter; use it only as a slow cross-check.
- **Fusion [E]:** on-chip fusion (LSM6DSV16X SFLP, BNO085) is convenient for heading, but a balance loop wants raw gyro at 1 kHz with your own complementary or Mahony filter on the ESP32. Treat on-chip fusion as a bonus, not a requirement.
- **Placement [E]:** mount the IMU on the main carrier PCB, rigidly, near the body's centre (under the phone holder), not on a dangling breakout. That is a strong reason to put the IMU chip on the custom board in the final design and use breakouts only for prototypes.

### Gaps
- No breakout dimensions found for the Adafruit LSM6DSO32 (#4692) or ISM330DHCX (#4502), the Seeed Grove BMI088, or any ready-made ICM-42688-P or ICM-45686 board from a major vendor. No SparkFun or Adafruit ICM-45686 breakout was found.
- The ICM-42688-P ODR maximum conflicts (6.4 kHz on one TDK-hosted page vs 32 kHz in Zephyr's API); the ICM-45686 gyro ODR is not found; LSM6DSV16X ODR and noise are not found; BMI323 ODR and noise are not found.
- The BNO085 accelerometer range and SPI clock come from weak sources only.
- Mounting-hole positions for these breakouts were not found (vendors rarely publish them, per a [SparkFun forum request](https://community.sparkfun.com/t/mechanical-drawings-for-breakout-boards-please/16139)).
- Driver licences (TDK, ST, Bosch) were not checked in this session.

At 100 Hz and a 0.2 m/s walk, the robot moves about 2 mm between readings.
- **Independent edge cut [E]:** wire a 5 cm Pololu digital IR sensor (or the Sharp, while stock lasts) to the safety MCU. Mount it so the floor sits at about 2–3 cm. If the floor falls away, or the sensor dies and its pull-up wins, the output goes "no object" and the motor power is cut. That direction is fail-safe. Caveat from general knowledge, unverified here: IR reflectance can miss very dark or shiny floors, so test on the owner's real desk surfaces.
- **Addresses [E]:** every ST ToF part starts at 0x29 (SparkFun's "0x52" is the 8-bit form of 0x29). With two or more ToF sensors, keep the XSHUT boot sequence already used in Pebble's firmware, or put each sensor on its own I²C bus (the S3 has two).

## 5. Power monitoring and fuel gauges

### Takeaway
Use a **TI INA228** (85 V, 20-bit, conversions from 50 µs, energy and charge accumulators, 75 µs ALERT) on the motor bus. Prototype on the Adafruit board (#5832, 25.8 × 20.2 × 10.1 mm, 3.3 g, 15 mΩ shunt, up to 10 A); in the final design, put the chip with a lower-value shunt on the carrier PCB. A cell fuel gauge (MAX17048) does not fit here, because the Xiaomi power bank is sealed; use the INA228's charge counter and voltage instead.

### Cited Findings
- **INA228:** 20-bit delta-sigma ADC; shunt input ±163.84 mV or ±40.96 mV; common mode −0.3 to +85 V; conversion time 50 µs–4.12 ms; averaging 1–1024; 2.7–5.5 V supply; I²C up to 2.94 MHz; alert response 75 µs; gain error ±0.05 % max, offset ±1 µV max; measures current, voltage, power, energy and charge. [O] [TI INA228 product page](https://ti.com/product/INA228); [O] [TI datasheet](https://ti.com/lit/gpn/INA228)
- **Adafruit INA228 #5832:** 25.8 × 20.2 × 10.1 mm, 3.3 g (Pi Hut listing); 15 mΩ 0.1 % shunt; up to 10 A (about 10 µA/LSB) or 2.75 A at about 2.5 µA/LSB; high or low side; 3.5 mm terminal block; STEMMA QT; 3–5 V logic. [S] [The Pi Hut](https://thepihut.com/products/adafruit-ina228-i2c-85v-20-bit-high-or-low-side-power-monitor-stemma-qt-qwiic); [O] [Adafruit](https://adafruit.com/product/5832)
- SparkFun's INA228 Qwiic board uses a 10 mΩ default shunt. [S] [The Pi Hut, SparkFun INA228](https://thepihut.com/products/sparkfun-current-sensor-ina228-qwiic). Adafruit also sells an INA238 board (#6349). [S] [Little Bird](https://littlebirdelectronics.com.au/products/adafruit-ina238-dc-current-voltage-power-monitor-ada6349)
- **MAX17048:** single lithium cell only; ModelGauge needs no sense resistor or learning cycles [O] [MAX17048 datasheet (SparkFun copy)](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/b/b/2/c/b/MAX17048.pdf); 2.5–4.5 V battery, I²C 0x36, about 3 µA quiescent (7Semi board) [S] [Hubtronics](https://hubtronics.in/7semi/max17048-fuel-gauge-breakout?page=9); board sizes 17.8 × 17.8 mm [S] [Cirkit Designer](https://docs.cirkitdesigner.com/component/2d86a0e7-cdc3-4a51-bade-c9ab17f98b2b/max17048-breakout) or 25.7 × 20.3 × 7.2 mm [S] [RoboFlock docs](https://roboflock-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/hardware_docs/max_17048.html).
- The UM TinyS3[D] has an I²C fuel gauge on board, in place of a VBAT ADC pin. [S] [Pimoroni TinyS3](https://shop.pimoroni.com/en-au/products/tinys3-esp32-s3)

### Inferences
- **Shunt sizing [E]:** with a 2 mΩ shunt on the ±40.96 mV range, full scale is ±20.5 A; on the ±163.84 mV range it is ±82 A. A 2 mΩ, 2512 or 2725 shunt on the carrier covers a 12-joint hexapod's peaks (the actual bus current is for the motor researcher). Adafruit's 15 mΩ board would saturate at about 10 A and drop 0.15 V at 10 A, so it is fine on the bench only.
- **Safety use [E]:** set the INA228 ALERT for over-current or under-voltage and wire it to the safety MCU as well as the ESP32. At 75 µs it is fast enough to be one of the cut inputs.
- **Battery state [E]:** the Xiaomi power bank's cell is not reachable, so a MAX17048-style gauge cannot see it. Use INA228 bus voltage under load plus its charge accumulator, reset at each full charge, to estimate the remaining charge. Report it in plain words ("about half left").

### Gaps
- No INA219 or INA226 breakout dimensions or specs were returned. From prior knowledge, unverified: INA219 is 26 V, 12-bit; INA226 is 36 V, 16-bit; INA228 supersedes both for this use.
- No Adafruit or SparkFun MAX17048 board dimensions were confirmed.

## 6. Independent safety circuit that cuts motor power (tip-over, drop edge, lost heartbeat)

### Takeaway
Build a small, separate, default-off safety stage: a **CH32V003** (48 MHz RISC-V with independent and window watchdogs, SOP-8 or TSSOP-20, about US$0.10 in volume per a launch claim) or a pure-hardware **TPS3430** window watchdog. It watches a toggling heartbeat from the ESP32, the binary nose edge sensor, its own tilt sensor and the INA228 ALERT, and drives the enable pin of a motor-power switch. For up to 5.5 A per branch, use a **TPS25947** eFuse (2.7–23 V, 28.3 mΩ, 2 × 2 mm); above that, use an external back-to-back MOSFET controller (**LTC4365** or **LM74502**). I found no off-the-shelf small-robot reference design; the pattern is well established in patents and drone and robot practice.

### Cited Findings
- **CH32V003:** up to 48 MHz, 2 KB SRAM, 16 KB flash; SOP-8 (CH32V003J4M6, 6 GPIO) and TSSOP-20 (CH32V003F4P6, 18 GPIO) among others; 2 watchdogs (independent and window). "Under 10 cents" comes from a WCH director's tweet, quantity unknown. A hobbyist reports an SPI bug on the SOP-16 variant. [S] [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2022/10/22/10-cents-ch32v003-risc-v-mcu-offers-2kb-sram-16kb-flash-in-sop8-to-qfn20-packages/); [S] [Hackster](https://hackster.io/arduinocelentano/the-cheapest-ch32v003-risc-v-development-board-3917d1); [S] [Components101](https://components101.com/ics/ch32v003-32-bit-general-purpose-risc-v-mcu)
- **TPS3430:** standalone window watchdog, 1.6–6.5 V, about 10 µA, open-drain active-low WDO (1–100 kΩ pull-up), 3 × 3 mm VSON-10. WDI needs a falling edge inside the window; WDI is ignored during power-up until tRST expires. SET0/SET1/CWD choose or disable the window; a capacitor on CRST sets the reset delay; ±2.5 % typical, ±15 % over −40 to 125 °C. [O] [TI TPS3430 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/tps3430); [O] [TI document viewer](https://ti.com/document-viewer/TPS3430/datasheet/GUID-AAE69F8A-3F22-4120-81A2-FFCC8633ECCC). A MikroE "Watchdog Click" (MIKROE-4416) appeared in the same results; its chip was not confirmed. [S] [The Debug Store](https://thedebugstore.com/products/watchdog-click-board-mikroe-4416-uk)
- **TPS25947 eFuse:** 2.7–23 V, 5.5 A, integrated back-to-back FETs, RON 28.3 mΩ typical, current limit adjustable 0.5–6 A, 2 × 2 mm 10-pin QFN, −40 to 125 °C junction. [O] [TI TPS25947](https://focus.ti.com/product/TPS25947); [S] [Mouser](https://www.mouser.pl/ti-tps25947-efuses)
- **LTC4365:** over-voltage, under-voltage and reverse-supply controller driving back-to-back N-MOSFETs; blocks +60 V and −40 V; SHDN pin turns the FETs off (10 µA shutdown, 125 µA running); FAULT output. [S] [Electronic Specifier](https://electronicspecifier.com/passives/linear-ltc4365-supply-protection-controller); [O] [Analog Devices article](https://www.analog.com/jp/resources/technical-articles/no-blocking-diode-needed-to-protect-sensitive-circuits.html); [O] [DN497](https://docs.ampnuts.ru/analog.com.datasheet/ltc4365/related_data/dn497f.pdf)
- **LM74502:** high-side controller for back-to-back external N-FETs (one for the load disconnect, one for reverse blocking), charge pump about 12 V above supply. [O] [TI SLVAFB3](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SLVAFB3). **TPS4811-Q1** family: 3.5–40 V smart high-side drivers for 12 V systems. [O] [TI SLUAAN7](https://ti.com/lit/SLUAAN7). The body diodes of back-to-back FETs block current both ways when off. [O] [TI SLUAA58](https://ti.com/lit/SLUAA58)
- **Patterns in practice:** a US patent titled "Independent safety processor for disabling the operation of high power devices" [O] [US 7253577](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7253577); a stepper controller keeps the stop logic "independent of the wifi chip", so the stop is immediate [S] [EEVblog](https://eevblog.com/forum/crowd-funded-projects/wi-fi-stepper-crowdsupply-campaign-is-go/msg2146657); a forum answer for small ESC robots: an MCU kill switch sets the PWM to neutral [S] [EEVblog](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/projects/help-with-switching-pwm-signals/).

### Inferences
- **Recommended architecture [E]:**
  1. **Inputs to the safety MCU:** (a) a heartbeat that must *toggle* inside a window (for example every 2–10 ms; a stuck-high or stuck-low pin, or a runaway loop toggling too fast, both count as lost); (b) the binary nose IR sensor (low = floor seen); (c) its own tilt sense, either a cheap separate accelerometer read by the safety MCU or an IMU interrupt line, but **not** the ESP32's word for it; (d) the INA228 ALERT; (e) an optional physical stop button.
  2. **Output:** EN of the motor-power switch, **pulled to off by a resistor**, so a dead, unflashed or browned-out safety MCU means no motor power. Ideally also hold the joint boards' own enable or brake lines low as a second path.
  3. **Latching:** after a cut, stay off until the ESP32 sends a deliberate re-arm (two distinct pulses) *and* all inputs are good. That matches Pebble's law-first stop handling in `brain/pebble/laws.py`.
- **Switch sizing [E]:** a TPS25947 per leg group (for example one per tripod, 2 × 5.5 A) is tiny and has current limiting built in. If the joints' combined peak exceeds about 10 A, use LTC4365 or LM74502 with two low-RDS(on) N-FETs. Put a TVS and bulk capacitance on the motor side: when power is cut while the legs move, FOC stages rectify back-EMF onto the bus, and the switch's reverse blocking leaves that energy nowhere else to go.
- **Pure-hardware option [E]:** TPS3430 WDO AND edge-sensor AND tilt-switch (a couple of logic gates) → eFuse EN. No firmware to get wrong, but no latching or re-arm logic unless you add a flip-flop. The CH32V003 gives that logic for pennies and is open to audit (its watchdog guards it in turn).
- **What a cut does [E]:** with power off, the legs go limp and a 140 × 80 mm body sinks onto its belly. That is safe at desk scale and better than walking off an edge. Gait code should still try a controlled crouch first; the hardware cut is the backstop.
- **Response time [E]:** with a CH32V003 polling at 10 kHz and an eFuse switching off in microseconds, the cut lands well under 1 ms after the edge sensor flips (2.56 ms sensor update). The total is set mostly by the sensor.

### Gaps
- No published small-robot schematic of exactly this pattern was found. The joint boards' own command-timeout behaviour (for example moteus) is the motor researcher's area.
- No current prices for TPS25947, TPS3430, LTC4365, LM74502 or CH32V003 were captured.
- TPS2595 and RP2040/ATtiny options were not researched further; the CH32V003 and TPS3430 cover the cheap-MCU and no-MCU ends.

## 7. Connectors and wiring for a small robot

### Takeaway
Use **JST-GH (1.25 mm)** for every signal cable on the moving body: CAN, I²C, sensors and the safety lines. It is the Pixhawk/DroneCAN standard (4-pin CAN: 5V, CAN_H, CAN_L, GND), so ready-made cables and peripherals exist. Keep **JST-SH/Qwiic (1.0 mm)** for bench prototyping and debug. Avoid mixing in **Molex PicoBlade**: it shares the 1.25 mm pitch but is a different, incompatible part. All three are rated about **1 A per pin**, so motor power needs a separate, larger connector.

### Cited Findings
- JST GH: 1.25 mm pitch, about 1 A per pin, 50 V, 30–26 AWG; JST SH: 1.0 mm, about 1 A per pin, 50 V, 32–28 AWG. [O] [JST core selection table](https://grobotronics.com/images/companies/1/datasheets/jst%20core%20selection.pdf)
- Molex PicoBlade 53047 header: 1.25 mm, 1 A rating (distributor). [S] [RS Online](https://my.rs-online.com/web/p/pcb-headers/4476603); comparison of PicoBlade and JST SH: [S] [Kyle Manna blog](https://blog.kylemanna.com/hardware/molex-picoblade-vs-jst-sh-connectors/)
- The Pixhawk connector standard uses JST-GH (1.25 mm) for peripherals and power and JST-SH (1.0 mm) for debug. ARK's 4-pin GH pinouts: CAN = 5V, CAN_H, CAN_L, GND; I²C = 5V, SCL, SDA, GND; red = power, black = ground, yellow = first data line. [S] [ARK Electron docs](https://docs.arkelectron.com/knowledge-base/knowledge-base/connectors-and-wiring)
- Pinouts vary: MLAB's GH4PIN01 uses GND-SDA-+5V-SCL-GND to survive reversed cables. Many Pixhawk clones used PicoBlade, which is "not compatible" with DF13 or GH. [S] [MLAB GH4PIN01](https://github.com/mlab-modules/GH4PIN01); [O] [PX4 docs: Pixhawk 1](https://docs.px4.io/v1.14/zh/flight_controller/pixhawk.html)
- Real-world small-robot choices found: the mjbots quad (moteus joints on CAN-FD through the pi3hat) [S] [hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/167845-mjbots-quad); Petoi's Quaddle (ESP32-S3, servos) and the open TNY-360 quadruped (hobby servos) [S] [Open Source For You](https://www.opensourceforu.com/?p=103892), [Open Source For You](https://www.opensourceforu.com/?p=103958).

### Inferences
- **Wiring plan [E]:** carrier board → 2 × 4-pin GH CAN ports (one per side, Pixhawk pinout, daisy-chained through the joint boards, 120 Ω at each far end); 1 × GH I²C to the nose (VL53L4CD); 1 × 3-pin GH to the nose IR sensor (to the safety MCU); 1 × GH for the stop button. Keep the I²C run to the nose short or slow (100–400 kHz). The current Pebble already runs I²C about 180 mm to its front sensors (`interfaces.md`).
- GH's latch (prior knowledge; SH and Qwiic hold by friction only) matters on a walking robot that shakes every step.
- Use 26 AWG silicone wire in GH for CAN and power-to-logic. Never run the joints' motor current through GH or SH (1 A/pin).

### Gaps
- No JST-GH, JST-SH or PicoBlade mating cycles, vibration ratings or official Molex PicoBlade current ratings were retrieved. The DS-009 Pixhawk standard PDF itself was not fetched.

## 8. Recommendation: main board, IMU, edge sensor, power monitor, safety circuit

### Takeaway
**Prototype now:** Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero (or a XIAO ESP32S3 Plus) on a hand-built carrier + Adafruit LSM6DSO32 over SPI (±32 g) + MCP2518FD SPI CAN-FD board(s) with TJA1051T/3-class transceivers (or the S3's own TWAI with a CAN Pal if the joints are classic CAN) + Pololu VL53L4CD + Pololu 5 cm digital IR + Adafruit INA228 + a CH32V003 driving a TPS25947 eFuse. **Publishable build:** one custom carrier PCB (about 60 × 40 mm, 4 × M2 holes), either taking the castellated Zero (easiest for others to build) or with an ESP32-S3-MINI-1-N4R2 soldered on. On it: an ICM-45686 at the body centre, 2 × MCP2518FD + FD transceivers on JST-GH, an INA228 with a 2 mΩ shunt, and the CH32V003 + eFuse/FET safety stage.

### Cited Findings
- Zero: 23.5 × 18 mm, castellated, ME6217C33M5G 800 mA LDO, 3.7–6 V input, about 24 GPIO. [O] [Waveshare docs](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Zero); [S] [Electrokit](https://www.electrokit.com/en/esp32-s3-dev-board-mini-8mb-psram-8mb-with-headers)
- XIAO ESP32S3 Plus: 21 × 17.8 mm, 16 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM, extra castellated GPIOs. [S] [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2025/01/13/seeed-studio-xiao-plus-series-adds-more-gpio-castellated-holes/)
- ESP32-S3-MINI-1: 15.4 × 20.5 × 2.4 mm; N4R2 = 4 MB flash + 2 MB PSRAM. [O] [Espressif datasheet](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-s3-mini-1_mini-1u_datasheet_en.pdf)
- TWAI is classic only and treats FD frames as errors. [O] [ESP-IDF TWAI](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/twai.html); MCP2518FD reaches 8 Mbit/s data. [S] [Mouser](https://www.mouser.com/new/microchip/microchip-mcp2518fd-controllers)
- ICM-45686: ±32 g, ±4000 dps, 3.8 mdps/√Hz. [O] [TDK](https://adm.invensense.tdk.com/icm-45686); LSM6DSO32 breakout: ±32 g, SPI, US$12.50. [O] [Adafruit #4692](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4692)
- VL53L4CD: 1 mm–1.2 m, 18°, 100 Hz; Pololu 13 × 18 × 2 mm, US$16.95. [O] [ST](https://www.st.com/en/imaging-and-photonics-solutions/vl53l4cd.html); [O] [Pololu #3692](https://www.pololu.com/product/3692)
- Sharp/Pololu 5 cm digital IR: 2.56 ms update, binary output. [O] [Pololu #1131](https://www.pololu.com/product/1131)
- INA228: 85 V, 20-bit, 75 µs alert. [O] [TI](https://ti.com/product/INA228); TPS25947: 5.5 A, 23 V, 2 × 2 mm. [O] [TI](https://focus.ti.com/product/TPS25947); CH32V003: independent + window watchdog. [S] [CNX](https://www.cnx-software.com/2022/10/22/10-cents-ch32v003-risc-v-mcu-offers-2kb-sram-16kb-flash-in-sop8-to-qfn20-packages/)

### Inferences
- **Main board [E]:** stay on the ESP32-S3, so the existing PlatformIO firmware and native tests carry over. Pick the **Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero** as the module: most GPIOs of the tiny boards, a known 800 mA LDO, a 3.7–6 V input that suits a 5 V power-bank rail, castellations for a flat carrier, and native USB-C straight to the phone. The XIAO ESP32S3 Plus is the alternative if more flash/PSRAM or Seeed's documentation matters more than pin count. Rule out the ESP32-S3-Tiny (no USB), the DevKitC-1 (size) and the QT Py/base XIAO (too few GPIOs for SPI + CAN + I²C + safety lines).
- **Motor bus [E]:** decide with the motor researcher's result. If the joints speak CAN-FD, use 2 × MCP2518FD (6 joints per bus) for a 1 kHz exchange. If classic CAN only, use the TWAI plus 1 × MCP2518FD in classic mode as a second bus, and expect about 250–400 Hz setpoints with the joints interpolating. Either way, the gait engine itself can tick at 1 kHz on the ESP32.
- **IMU [E]:** final choice **ICM-45686** on the carrier (±32 g headroom for landings, ±4000 dps, low noise, low power). Fall back to the **ICM-42688-P** if measured landing peaks stay well under 16 g and driver maturity matters more. Use the **BMI088** if vibration from 12 BLDC joints proves troublesome. Prototype with the **Adafruit LSM6DSO32** over SPI to measure the real peaks before freezing the PCB.
- **Edge sensor [E]:** **Pololu VL53L4CD** in the nose for distance (to the ESP32 over I²C, at Pebble's familiar address scheme), plus a **5 cm Pololu digital IR** sensor wired only to the safety MCU. The two use different physics (laser ToF vs IR triangulation) and different processors, so no single failure blinds both.
- **Power monitor [E]:** **INA228** with a 2 mΩ shunt on the carrier (±20 A on the fine range), ALERT to both the ESP32 and the safety MCU; the Adafruit #5832 for bench work.
- **Safety [E]:** **CH32V003** (TSSOP-20, for spare pins) with its independent watchdog, default-off EN, latched trip, and a deliberate re-arm. Switch with **TPS25947** eFuses per leg group if per-group peaks are ≤5.5 A, otherwise **LTC4365/LM74502 + back-to-back N-FETs**. Add a TVS and bulk capacitance on the motor side. A **TPS3430** can serve as a second, firmware-free watchdog on the same EN line.
- **Connectors [E]:** JST-GH everywhere on the body with Pixhawk/DroneCAN pinouts; Qwiic only on the bench.
- **Licences [E]:** the chips themselves carry no licence issue. Adafruit and SparkFun breakouts are CC BY-SA design files (share-alike applies only if you copy their PCB designs into Pebble's). Check arduino-esp32's LGPL-2.1 status and the driver licences for the chosen IMU before publishing.

### Gaps
- The final choice between one and two CAN-FD buses depends on the joint boards' protocol (broadcast frames or not) and their FD support, which is outside this brief.
- No measured landing g-levels exist yet for this body; the IMU range choice should be confirmed by a drop test with the ±32 g prototype IMU.
- Prices in NZD and current (October 2026) USD prices for the chosen ICs were not obtained.
